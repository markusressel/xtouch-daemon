Architecture¶

How the daemon works inside, for developers. To set up the project and run the tests, see Contributing.

graph LR
    Device[X-Touch Extender<br/>/dev/snd/midiC*D0] <-- "raw MIDI (midi.py, protocol.py)" --> Surface
    Surface -- "events: fader, touch, buttons, encoders" --> Desk
    Desk -- "to the controller bound to the strip" --> Mixer[SlotMixer]
    Mixer -- "set_fader, set_text, set_led ..." --> Surface
    Backend[PulseBackend<br/>one pulsectl-asyncio connection] -- "AudioState snapshot after every change" --> Mixer
    Mixer -- "set volume, mute, default" --> Backend
    Engine[SlotEngine<br/>slots/] -- "what each slot shows" --> Mixer
    Backend <--> Sound[pipewire-pulse]
    Desk -- "captures (meter.py), set_meter" --> PwRecord[pw-record, passive]
    Mixer -- "entities of channels, states, slots" --> Bridge[MqttBridge<br/>aiomqtt]
    Bridge <-- "discovery, states, commands, events" --> Broker[MQTT broker / Home Assistant]
    Daemon -- "owns, reloads, stops" --> Surface & Backend & Mixer & Bridge
    Surface -- "what the strips show (live.py)" --> Live[LiveServer<br/>$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/xtouch-daemon.sock]
    Live -- "a JSON line per change" --> GUI[xtouch-daemon gui]
    GUI -- "mode, override, preview" --> Live
    Daemon -- "steps (runtime/slots.py)" --> Engine
    Voip[VoipProvider<br/>voip/] -- "participants" --> Engine & Mixer

Modules¶

The packages of src/xtouch_daemon/, from the device up. Tests mirror them: tests/<package>/test_<module>.py.

Module Role
hardware/protocol.py The Mackie Control codec: decodes the device's MIDI messages into events, builds the messages that drive it. Pure functions.
hardware/midi.py Finds the device's sound card in /proc/asound/cards and reads and writes its raw MIDI device without blocking.
hardware/strip.py Strip: what a strip of the device shows, sending only what changed; its motor fader glides to its target and stays still while touched. VirtualStrip: a strip that waits off the device.
hardware/transport.py How the Surface reaches the device: a Port finds and opens it, AlsaPort the raw MIDI device of its sound card, SocketPort the virtual device's Unix socket.
hardware/surface.py Surface: the connection to the device (connects again after it was unplugged), its events, touches, and the startup and shutdown sequences. Shows everything again after a reconnect. Keeps a SimulatedDevice as the mirror of the device (what it sent, what the device reported), which the live view shows.
hardware/selftest.py xtouch-daemon test: lights up the device and echoes what happens on it.
audio/model.py AudioState: an immutable snapshot of the outputs, inputs, application streams and defaults.
audio/backend.py AudioBackend: the interface the strips use (snapshots, volume, mute, default).
audio/pulse.py PulseBackend: one connection to the sound server that follows it by events and carries out the commands.
audio/meter.py Meter: audio levels from passive pw-record captures.
audio/media.py The media players of the session (MPRIS), for encoder = "media".
config/ The TOML configuration, validated with pydantic: files (reading it, its errors), model (Config), sections, slots (the channels, states and slots, with the strip of every candidate), the types of the channels' strips (strip, strips, audio_strips, voip_strips), values, names (lists with presets) and presets (@browsers, @steam-games, ..., expanded when the configuration is validated, see Design: presets).
strips/ The strip controllers: DeviceStrip (and ChoiceStrip for choices), AppGroupStrip, AutoStrip (the application of an auto slot), VoipStrip (a person), HomeAssistantStrip, EmptyStrip, and the shared VolumeStrip base. A controller gets its Identity (the ids and names of its entities in Home Assistant, its name in the log) and its Look (label, color, meter, LEDs) from outside: the binder makes them per channel and candidate (look_of()).
slots/ Design: channels, states and slots without the device: the model (model), conditions (expressions), presence (inputs), the delays (timing), the states and modes (states), the resolver (resolver), the slots over time (simulation), the distinct outcomes (outcomes), recorded observations (recording) and their replay (replay). Pure but for the recorder; the daemon drives it through the SlotEngine, simulate and observe too.
mqtt.py MqttBridge: the connection to the MQTT broker for homeassistant channels and controls. Announces their entities (haad-mqtt-autodiscovery), restores the retained states, passes commands on, publishes availability.
runtime/daemon.py Daemon: the lifecycle (signals, reload, shutdown, systemd notifications), stepping the slots and the requests for modes and overrides.
runtime/desk.py Desk: the strips of the device for as long as the daemon runs. Hands each event to the controller (Presenter) bound to its strip, a release to whoever got the press, follows the hands on the device and the chord of Solo buttons. Its meters (one per strip of the device) capture what the controllers bound now show, so a capture goes on when another slot shows the same. A claim (claim(), the [controls] of a slot) takes the controls it names: their events go to it, and the VirtualStrip of the presenter leaves their fader, ring and LEDs to it (mask()).
runtime/controls.py Controls: the [controls] of the slots, built once per configuration with their entities in Home Assistant (number.<ns>_slot_<n>_fader, ..., or the id instead of slot_<n>). After each step the first entry of a slot whose when holds is its claim on the Desk (a slot under a finger keeps its claim until it is let go); it shows what it takes and launches, picks modes (SlotEngine.choose_mode(), cycle_modes()), toggles states and modes (toggle()), or sends events to Home Assistant.
runtime/homeassistant.py SlotEntities: with [mqtt], the mode select, a binary sensor, override select and (homeassistant = true) input switch per state, whether each channel is shown, and what each slot shows. Published after each step when they changed (all of them again after the broker was away); choices in Home Assistant go to the SlotEngine, inputs into its observation.
runtime/slots.py, runtime/binder.py The slots on the device. config/slots builds and checks the strip of every candidate when the file is loaded; the SlotEngine steps a Simulation 0.1 s after a change (the audio, the voice chat, a finger let go), at the deadlines of its delays and every 2 s for the processes; it takes the requests (a mode, an override, the pick and pin of a slot's pick encoder) and hands out the applications of the auto strips, keeps overrides, the mode picked (slots.json) and the people (people.json) across restarts (runtime/slot_files.py), and carries its state over a reload by id. The SlotMixer (runtime/binder.py) has a controller per channel for as long as the configuration, one per application of auto and per person without a channel while they are members, and an empty one per slot; after each step it binds to the Desk only the slots whose result changed (the others keep their controller, texts, fader and LEDs) and gives a controller the look, encoder and volume range of the candidate it is on.
simulator/ The virtual device: device (SimulatedDevice, the model of the device without IO: what the host sets, a hand on it, the motors and meters over time, a broken touch sensor, switching it off and on) and server (it on a Unix socket for virtual-device). The GUI hosts it with Qt (gui/qt/virtual_device.py). See Design: device simulator.
system/processes.py The processes that run, from /proc, for the processes of states and launch.
system/launch.py Launcher: starts the command of a launch (of a channel or a control) detached from the daemon (a transient systemd user service); LaunchButton: when Select launches, and its LED. See Design: launching applications.
system/instance.py The running daemon for other processes: its runtime file (process, configuration file) and where its live view is served.
system/systemd.py systemd integration: notify, journal log levels, the unit file, controlling the user service.
live/ The live view for the GUI over a Unix socket: messages (snapshots, requests, replies), codec (lines of JSON), server (LiveServer). See Live view.
voip/ VoipProvider: the people of a voice chat, with DemoProvider and BridgeProvider (a client plugin on a Unix socket).
voip/mumble.py, integrations/mumble/ The Mumble plugin (C): its sources, building its bundle with CMake, finding it and copying it into Mumble's plugin directory. See Mumble.
cli/ The command line: one module per topic (daemon, configuration, sound, modes, slots, mumble, service, editor, simulator), put together by main in cli/__init__.py.
gui/domain/ The configuration editor without a toolkit: the editable document (slots_document, history, changes), the options of the models (schema), the stacks of the slots (slot_stacks), the look of the strips (looks), suggestions from the sound server (sources), where a problem is shown (problems), applying a saved file (apply). See GUI.
gui/qt/ The widgets of xtouch-daemon gui (the gui extra): window/ (the main window, its rail, the window Device, the service, the filter of the keys and the wheel), preview/ (the drawing of the device), pages/ (a module per page; the cards of the page Slots in slot_cards), fields/, theme/.

Data flow¶

  1. PulseBackend subscribes to sink, source, sink input and server events. After a burst of events it takes one snapshot (AudioState) and hands it to the mixer.
  2. The SlotEngine resolves the slots (0.1 s after a change); the SlotMixer binds the slots whose result changed and calls update(state) on every controller. A controller shows the state on its Strip: color, LCD lines, LEDs, ring and fader.
  3. The Surface sends only what changed. A fader target starts a glide that moves the motor every 10 ms.
  4. An event from the device goes from the Surface to the Desk, which hands it to the controller bound to its strip. A controller turns it into commands to the backend. These run as tasks: nothing blocks the event loop.
  5. The sound server reports the result as a new snapshot, which goes through step 2 again.

A homeassistant channel ignores the snapshots. Its entities work as in haad: Home Assistant sends a command, the entity publishes it as its state (retained), and the copy that comes back from the broker changes the entity; the bridge then tells the strip, which shows it. The device's events go the other way: a fader or encoder publishes its number (a fader moved by hand at most every 100 ms), buttons publish events, which are not retained.

Lifecycle¶

  • Start: the daemon starts the surface, the backend and the mixer as tasks and tells systemd READY=1. The device and the sound server do not need to be there: both are connected (again) when they appear.
  • Reload (SIGHUP): the configuration is read again. A file with errors is logged and the current configuration stays. Otherwise a new SlotMixer takes over the same surface and backend and gets the current state right away; the SlotEngine carries its states, picks and people over by id, so slots that show the same keep it. systemd learns about it through RELOADING=1 and READY=1.
  • Preview: a configuration of the GUI (see Live preview) takes over like a reload, from memory instead of the file. A reload ends it: the file runs, or the configuration of the file before when the file can't be used.
  • Stop (SIGTERM, SIGINT): the daemon tells systemd STOPPING=1, closes the mixer, turns every strip dark and moves the faders down, waits for the faders (at most 2 s), sends what is still queued and closes the device. A dark surface shows that nothing drives it anymore.
  • Stop, in this order: first everything stops that could show something (the sound server connection, MQTT, the mixer, the device's events), then the strips are reset, and only then the surface closes. A snapshot that arrived during the reset would otherwise paint the strips again.
  • MQTT: the bridge connects (again) on its own, like the backend. On every connect it publishes the discovery configurations, subscribes, and waits for the retained states with a ping on its own sync topic: the broker sends retained messages before later ones. Entities without a retained state then get their defaults. A reload hands the bridge the entities of the new channels; entities of removed channels are removed from Home Assistant (not by a preview, see Live preview). A changed [mqtt] section needs a restart.
  • Status: systemctl --user status xtouch-daemon shows whether the device, the sound server and (with [mqtt]) the broker are connected.

Design decisions¶

  • One snapshot for all strips. Every strip sees the same consistent state, and one connection serves eight strips. The strips derive everything from the snapshot instead of keeping their own copies.
  • Coalesced volume commands. A fader sends many positions per second. A strip sets only the latest one when the previous command finished, and shows the new value at once, so the display does not lag behind the hand.
  • Passive, native captures for the meters. The meters use pw-record with node.passive = true: a capture never wakes a device, never lights the microphone indicator, and never switches Bluetooth headphones into headset mode. They are native PipeWire streams on purpose: WirePlumber 0.5.18 (PipeWire 1.6.10) restores captures of PulseAudio clients (parec, peak detection streams) at 0 % volume, so those would only get silence. state.restore-props = false keeps WirePlumber from saving the captures at all. node.dont-reconnect = true keeps a capture with its device or stream: WirePlumber makes a stream whose target is the default when it is linked follow the default (like PulseAudio), so without it the meter of the default output would show the next default output after Select. pw-record runs under stdbuf -o0 (coreutils): its buffered output comes in blocks of 4096 bytes (64 ms at 8 kHz), so the meters lagged, and two meters of the same audio differed by up to that much. Unbuffered, it writes every cycle of the graph; the daemon takes the peak of every 20 ms.
  • Meter points from the volume. Measured with PipeWire 1.6.10 (a tone into a null sink, the volume and Mute of the test stream and sink set with pw-cli set-param): a playback stream and an input apply their volume and Mute in their own node, in audioconvert's channel mixer, before their output ports, so every capture of them is after both. audioconvert has monitor ports only on nodes with input ports (outputs, capture streams), not on playback streams or inputs, so there is no tap before the volume to link to; node.passive changes only the scheduling. The monitor of an output is before its volume and Mute (monitorVolumes and monitorMute, both untouched, unless monitor.channel-volumes is on). pipewire-pulse turns a PulseAudio volume v into the linear channel volume v³ (50 % is 0.125). So the meters capture what they can and scale it with the volume the sound server tells (meter.scale_of()): pre-fader divides the audio of a stream or input by v³, post-fader multiplies the monitor of an output by it (0 while muted). The audio of a muted stream or input is gone, its pre-fader level with it. The volume news is ahead of the captured audio, so after a change the division uses the loudest of the volumes of the last 0.3 s (GAIN_SETTLE): a fader pulled down must not light the clip LED. Reference: spa/plugins/audioconvert/audioconvert.c (monitor ports in reconfigure_mode() only for SPA_DIRECTION_INPUT; the monitor volume in impl_node_process()).
  • Stall restart. WirePlumber links a passive capture only once and gives up when that fails, e.g. while Bluetooth headphones disappear and streams move. A capture that gets no audio for 3 s while its device or stream plays is started again.
  • The default device follows by name. A strip without device looks up the default in every snapshot, so it follows a switch of the default device, including its meter.
  • One owner per strip. A strip belongs to PipeWire or to Home Assistant, chosen by its type. Mixing both on one fader would make two sources fight over the motor.
  • Availability follows the device. The entities are available while the daemon runs and the device is connected, so Home Assistant greys out controls that would do nothing. A last will covers a crash.
  • haad-mqtt-autodiscovery as a path dependency. The entities are the same as haad's, used from a checkout next to this one (like haad does), which also sets the Python version to 3.14.
  • No volume restore in the daemon. The sound server keeps the volumes. The daemon remembers only the volume of an idle application strip, for its next stream, and only while it runs.

GUI¶

xtouch-daemon gui is an optional editor for the configuration file, in the subpackage gui and the extra of the same name, like the GUI of openhasp-config-manager. The daemon itself does not import it.

  • Toolkit-free domain, thin widgets. gui/domain decides everything: SlotsDocument holds the file as plain values, validates it with parse_config() (the same messages as check-config), and saves it. slot_stacks turns the stacks of the slots into rows to edit, simulate and the outcomes of the slots what other modes and states would show, looks how the live view draws each strip, status whether the device runs the saved file, sources turns sound server snapshots into suggestions, problems which page shows a problem, apply() decides between reload, restart and "the daemon does not run". gui/qt only shows that and passes edits back.
  • Pages by what they edit. A rail on the left of the window Configuration (gui/qt/window/rail.py) has the pages: Slots (a card per slot with its stack, an inspector for the candidate picked, why it shows, Simulate), Channels, States and Settings ([device], [behavior], [aliases], [mqtt] as "Home Assistant", [voip]). Slots, Channels and States are two panes with a PaneSplitter (gui/qt/window/splitter.py) between them, which keeps its share in QSettings.
  • The device on every page. The drawing of the device (DeviceView, gui/qt/window/device_view.py) and the pages with their rail (the window Configuration) are QDockWidgets: one above the other at first, at any side, or windows of their own; QMainWindow.saveState() keeps where they are. The central widget is an empty placeholder, so the docks have all the room. The header and the footer are fixed QToolBars, so they reach across the window and the docks sit between them. A click on a strip of the drawing picks its slot on the page Slots. The menus hold what is used now and then (the theme, the zoom) and the keys of the window.
  • Previews of the controls. gui/domain/demos.py groups the options of a channel by the part of the strip they are about and makes a short scene for each part from the values in the form (frames of a StripState with a caption). PartPreview (gui/qt/preview/part_view.py) shows its steps (‹ and ›) in the same StripView as the window Device, cut to the part; a StripView is drawn in any StripHost. See Design: previews of the controls.
  • Undo instead of questions. History (gui/domain/history.py) keeps the states of the document before every edit, so removing a channel or reverting the file asks nothing and Ctrl+Z brings it back. Typing into one option is one step. Ctrl+Z undoes in the document also while a text field has the focus (gui/qt/window/input_filter.py, which also keeps the wheel from changing a field under the mouse).
  • Forms from the models. gui/domain/schema.py reads the options of every section and channel type from the pydantic models: the type picks the widget (switch, number, choice, color swatches, list chips, masked secret, file), the attribute docstrings become the help, Field constraints the ranges, and Meanings next to a choice (config/values.py, e.g. INDICATORS) say what each value does; Presets on a list of names (StreamNames) makes it suggest the presets of its kind. A new option or channel type needs no GUI code; a test fails for an option without help or a value without a meaning. gui/domain/docs.py holds the "Learn more" links into this site, and a test checks each against the headings of docs/.
  • The comments stay. Saving writes the changes into the TOML document with tomlkit: unchanged values keep their place, their comments and their form (a stack as a string, a list or tables), and a renamed channel keeps its table. The file is written in place, so a symlinked configuration stays one.
  • The daemon is found, not guessed. xtouch-daemon run writes $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/xtouch-daemon.json (its process id, its configuration file, whether systemd started it) while it runs (system/instance.py), so the GUI finds it wherever it was started, and a second daemon names the one that has the device. A saved file is reloaded (SIGHUP) only when the daemon reads that file; a changed [mqtt] needs a restart, since a reload does not take it (another [device] name or virtual it does: Surface.use). The GUI restarts only the service: systemd.Service (shared with install-service) reads the state of the unit, and installs, starts and restarts it.
  • PyQt6, QtAwesome and qt-themes. The same as openhasp-config-manager, so the tools look and work alike. The wheels carry Qt itself, so uv tool install needs no system packages (GTK 4 through PyGObject needs the system libraries and a compiler), Qt paints the strip preview with QPainter and does drag and drop, and pytest-qt runs the widgets offscreen.
  • One visual system. qt/theme/ holds the design tokens: the colors of the dark and the light theme (text at 4.5:1 or more and the outlines of controls at 3:1 or more on every surface, WCAG 2.2 AA, checked by the tests), a 4 px spacing scale, radii, the height of controls and a type scale relative to the desktop font, and the colors of the device for the drawing, the same in both themes. Widgets set style roles (theme.set_role) and take their sizes from the tokens instead of styling themselves. The drawing decides what a strip shows (a StripFace: LCD, ring, LEDs, meter, fader) apart from painting it.
  • One source of truth: the Editor. qt/editor.py holds the document, its history and its validation. Views read from it and change the document only through it, so nothing keeps a copy that could go stale. Its signal changed(Change) comes once per edit, after the document was validated once.
  • Changes say what changed. A Change (gui/domain/changes.py) has a Scope and, for some scopes, a path:
  • VALUE: a value in place; the field that made the change shows it already.
  • CHANNELS, STATES: a channel or state was added, removed, renamed or duplicated.
  • SLOTS: a candidate of a stack was added, removed, moved or changed.
  • SECTION: a section was added or removed.
  • ALIASES: the list of aliases.
  • DOCUMENT: anything (undo, redo, revert).
  • FILE: the file was written, so the document is the same. Each page's show_document(change) updates only what that scope touches.
  • Built once, updated in place. The pages build their widgets once, and showing the document loads the values into them (OptionRow.load()); no page is cleared and built again. A part that comes and goes (a section that is absent, the form of each channel type) is a page of a Stack that is sized to its current part, not a widget that is shown and hidden.
  • No layout for what stays the same. Showing the document again, or a live update 20 times a second, changes no geometry when nothing differs:
  • set_text and the tab texts are set only when they differ.
  • theme.set_role restyles only for another role.
  • The list chips stay while their values are the same. Icons are kept with their name and color (theme.set_icon), so switching the theme draws them again (theme.refresh_icons) without building a page.
  • The options in the order of the device. A channel's form lists its options from top to bottom like its strip:
  • what it controls (device, match, profiles, the id of a Home Assistant strip)
  • the encoder and its ring
  • the LCD
  • the meter
  • the Rec, Solo, Mute and Select buttons
  • the fader and the range of the volume
  • the id This is STRIP_OPTION_ORDER in gui/domain/schema.py, apart from the order of the fields in config/. A test fails for an option without a place in it.

Live view¶

The GUI shows the strips as the device shows them right now, which only the daemon knows. xtouch-daemon run serves that on the Unix socket $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/xtouch-daemon.sock (mode 0600, next to the runtime file of system/instance.py) for as long as it runs:

  • Whole states, one JSON line each. A client gets the whole state when it connects and again whenever something changed, checked 20 times a second (live.INTERVAL): the LCD lines and backlight, the LEDs, the ring, where the fader is and whether it is touched, the meter level, whether the device, the sound server and the broker are connected, whether the device is the virtual one, and the fingerprint() of the configuration in use. The strips are the Surface's mirror of the device (a SimulatedDevice, see hardware/surface.py): what reached it, with the motors and meters moving as on the device. A line is about 2 KB; a state rather than changes means a client that connects late or misses one needs nothing else. version changes when a line means something else.
  • Requests of the user only. A client may send requests, a line of JSON each: the live preview, a mode and an override (see below). The daemon takes them only from processes of its own user (SO_PEERCRED: root can connect to a socket of mode 0600), and a line of at most MAX_REQUEST (1 MiB): a longer one ends the connection. Otherwise the GUI changes the daemon through the configuration file and a reload.
  • Channels, states and slots. The state has slots (SlotsState): the mode and the one picked by hand, per slot the candidate, channel, pool, member and why, per state its value, since, source and override, and per pool the members that wait. {"request": "mode", "mode": "gaming"} (or auto, "+1") picks a mode, {"request": "override", "state": "call", "value": "on"} (auto, on, off) overrides a state. features has mode and override.
  • A hand from the GUI. {"request": "play", "event": "fader_moved", "strip": 2, "value": 8000} (also fader_touched with touched, button_pressed with button (its note) and pressed, encoder_turned with steps, encoder_pressed with pressed) is taken by the Surface as if the device sent it (Surface.play()), while a device is connected. No answer. features has play.
  • Never in the way. Clients get only non-blocking writes. One that falls behind by more than MAX_BUFFER is dropped (it can connect again), and a socket that can't be made is logged: the daemon runs on without the live view. The server is not one of the parts of the daemon whose end stops it.
  • Applied or not. The fingerprint is a hash of the whole configuration (secrets included). The GUI compares it with the one of the saved file, so it can say whether the daemon took the file, instead of assuming a reload worked (it keeps the old configuration when the new one has errors or a changed [mqtt]).

The GUI reads the socket with a QLocalSocket, without a thread, and connects again while the daemon runs. Without a daemon it shows what the file would show, and says so.

Live preview¶

The GUI can make the daemon run the configuration in its editor before it is saved (gui/qt/live_preview.py, the daemon side in Daemon.preview()):

  • The whole text, over the live view. StartPreview carries the TOML text the GUI would save (SlotsDocument.render()). The daemon reads it with parse_config(), the parser of the file, so the errors are the same as those of check-config, and runs it like a reload: a new mixer on the same surface, sound server and broker. The file is never written. A text with errors, or a changed [mqtt] (the bridge would need a new connection), is answered with the reason, and what runs keeps running. The GUI sends 300 ms after the last edit, and only a document without problems.
  • Replies the old GUI ignores, features the new one checks. The daemon answers with lines without version ({"reply": "preview", "ok": ..., "message": ...}), which a GUI from before skips like lines of another version. Snapshots tell preview (the daemon runs a preview, and config is its fingerprint) and features (preview, …); a daemon from before tells none and ignores requests, so the GUI keeps the switch off for it.
  • Always back to the file. The preview ends with EndPreview, when the connection of its client ends (the window closed or crashed), without a request for PREVIEW_TIMEOUT (10 s; the GUI sends KeepPreview every 3 s, so a window that hangs with its connection open ends it too), and when the file is loaded: on a reload (saving in the GUI sends one), and when the file changes on disk (checked every second during a preview). The client is told why ({"reply": "ended", ...}). The configuration of the file is kept in memory (Daemon.file_config), so a file that became unusable meanwhile does not keep the preview running.
  • One preview at a time. A second client takes it over, and the first one is told: the window someone works in wins over one that was left open.
  • Visible. The status line of systemd ends with "previewing unsaved changes of the GUI", the log tells when a preview starts and ends and why, and the GUI shows a pill.
  • Home Assistant entities survive a preview. A reload removes the entities of removed channels from Home Assistant (an empty retained discovery message), which also deletes them from its entity registry. A preview doesn't: MqttBridge.set_entities(..., preview=True) only stops driving the entities it does not have, which stay in Home Assistant with their retained states, so the file restores them as they were. The next configuration that is no preview removes what it does not have, like a reload; entities that only a preview had also lose the retained state they left on the broker, so a preview leaves nothing behind.
  • Secrets stay with the user. The text carries the secrets of the file. The GUI sends it only to a daemon of its own user (SO_PEERCRED on its end of the socket), and the runtime directory is the user's own ($XDG_RUNTIME_DIR, or a directory of mode 0700 in the temporary directory).
  • States and modes too. A preview is a whole configuration: the daemon runs it like a reload, carrying the states, overrides and the mode picked over by id, and the mode select in Home Assistant gets the modes of the preview.
  • A situation too. StartPreview may carry a situation (the what-if of Simulate, slots.outcomes.Situation), and its config may be None (the file's). The SlotEngine runs it in a second Simulation (what_if) that starts where the real one is: the states are overrides, the mode is picked, and the sound server and voice chat seen are made for it (Situation.observation: real stand-ins first, made-up ones for the rest). engine.active is what the mixer and the live view use; the real Simulation waits, takes the requests (the chord, Home Assistant, overrides), and is what Home Assistant sees: its slot sensors are not updated during a situation. Nothing of it is written (slots.json, people.json). Snapshots tell simulated; the feature is situation. It ends with the preview. See Design: try changes and situations on the device.

Channels, states and slots¶

The daemon runs a configuration of channels, states and slots (design).

  • One controller per channel, as long as the configuration. The SlotMixer (runtime/binder.py) builds a controller with its own VirtualStrip for every channel, and one for each application of auto and each person without a channel while they are members. After each step it binds to the Desk only the slots whose result changed: the others keep their controller, texts, fader and LEDs, and their meter capture goes on.
  • The engine decides, the mixer shows. The SlotEngine (runtime/slots.py) is the only one that hands out applications and people; the pick encoder and pins are requests to it (EngineSlot). It keeps the overrides, the mode picked and the order of people in the state directory (slots.json, people.json).
  • Under a finger nothing changes. Slots held on the Desk are fixed inputs of the resolver: a slot keeps what it shows, and its claims, until it is let go.
  • Feedback. A slot that shows something else flashes its label and why for switching.banner seconds; a new mode shows its banner across the LCDs instead.

Testing¶

The unit tests run without hardware and without a sound server:

  • the protocol codec and the MIDI parser with captured messages,
  • the raw MIDI device against a FIFO,
  • the surface with a recording send, and end to end against the SimulatedDevice in memory (tests/support/simulator.py, on the fake clock of the test) and over its socket,
  • the strips, the mixer and the daemon with a fake AudioBackend,
  • PulseBackend with a fake pulsectl connection,
  • the meters with a fake pw-record script,
  • the MQTT bridge and the homeassistant strips with an in-memory broker (tests/support/mqtt.py): retained messages, subscriptions, last wills, lost connections,
  • the live view with a real Unix socket in a short temporary directory: clients that read, that don't read, and that go away, requests of the user, of other users and that are too long, and the live preview through the daemon,
  • the live preview of the GUI against a fake live view (a QLocalServer) that answers like the daemon,
  • the Mumble plugin built with CMake and loaded into a fake Mumble (integrations/mumble/test/fake_mumble.c), which calls its callbacks as a script says, against a real BridgeProvider (skipped without cmake and a C compiler),
  • the GUI domain with configuration files in a temporary directory and a fake systemctl, and the GUI widgets with pytest-qt on the offscreen platform, with a fake sound server and the dialogs answered by the tests.

The tests mirror the packages of the source (tests/config/, tests/strips/, tests/gui/qt/, ...). Fixtures are in the conftest.py files: tests/conftest.py has the guards every test gets (no processes left behind, nothing of the machine touched, no programs started) and the common fakes, the packages have their own (e.g. the window in tests/gui/qt/conftest.py). What tests import, fakes and helpers, is in tests/support/, never in a conftest.py or another test module.
