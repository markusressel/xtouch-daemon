Design: channels, states and slots¶

Status: decided, being built. It replaces strips, layouts, inherit strips and collapse with concepts that are each edited in one place; the old format goes (see Decisions after the live test). The configuration format may change freely before 0.1.0. Revision 5, after three reviews of the design and a live test of a prototype on the device, see Review log. The resolver and the state engine run in xtouch_daemon.slots and xtouch-daemon simulate; the daemon runs them as a prototype, see Prototype.

Motivation¶

A [[strip]] today answers four questions at once, and the answers are spread over several mechanisms:

Question Today
What does it control? type and its options (device, match, choices, person, ...).
When does it matter? Implicitly ("has nothing to control"), collapse, the rules of a [[layout]] (apps, processes).
How is it related to other strips? inherit (by layout and position), the voip group, nothing for "these two belong together".
Where is it on the device? Its position in a list, shifted by collapse, replaced as a whole by a layout.

That makes simple wishes hard:

  • "VoIP and the Mic only during a call, else auto strips there" needs a group that collapses together, and extra auto strips that fill in. Neither exists.
  • "The VoIP strip matters while gaming and while calling" needs it in two layouts (an inherit strip) and a rule per layout, and the two can't both be active.
  • inherit refers to "strip 6 of the layout default": moving a strip silently changes what the reference means. The author's own configuration shows it: its gaming layout inherits strip 6, an auto strip, where VoIP was meant, and puts Browser where the Mic was, so the Mic is gone while gaming.
  • collapse moves every strip after it: the fader under the finger changes, which hurts muscle memory.
  • The GUI has to show all of it in one strip form, plus a "Layout" tab, plus global pages: it feels unintuitive.

The model¶

Concept Answers Edited on
Channel What a strip controls: a device, applications, a person in the voice chat, a Home Assistant strip. Channels page
Pool Which one of several a strip takes: whatever plays and has no slot (auto), the next present channel of a list. Channels page
People The people in the voice chat: the pool people, and with the applications the pool auto (see people-slots.md). Channels page (look, from and order); the learned order is live there
Controls What a fader, encoder or button of a slot does whatever the slot shows (a Home Assistant knob, starting Mumble). Device page
State When something matters: a named on/off condition, live, with a manual override. A mode is a state that excludes the other modes (today's layouts). States page
Slot Where: one of the 8 strips of the device, with a stack of candidates (channels or pools, each with a condition). Device page

Names¶

Channels, pools, states and the ids of controls share one namespace: an id is lower case letters, digits and _, unique across all four (a control id names Home Assistant entities like a channel id does). auto, people and default are built in. Reserved: and, or, not, if, present, none, mode, and ids starting with state_ or slot_ (they would collide with the entity IDs in Home Assistant). The sigils stay those of today: @name a preset, #name a tag.

Conditions¶

One expression language says "when" everywhere: in candidates ("mic if call") and in composed states (when = "...").

expr     := or
or       := and ("or" and)*
and      := not ("and" not)*
not      := "not" not | atom
atom     := ID | "present" | "present(" ID ")" | "(" expr ")"
  • ID: a state or mode; default is on while no other mode is.
  • present: the candidate's own channel or pool is present; only in candidates of slots (a state takes channels with its source channels). present(spotify): that channel or pool is. Presence of a pool is defined before slots are assigned (below), so conditions never depend on the result.
  • The GUI shows conditions as chips that read as text: call or gaming.
  • Errors point at the place: slots.7[0]: "voip if cal": unknown state cal (did you mean call?), with a caret in the GUI and check-config.

Conditions depend only on inputs: states and presence. Nothing in a condition depends on what other slots show. That is what makes the resolver deterministic and Simulate truthful.

Channels: what a strip controls¶

A channel is today's strip without its place: type and the options of that type. The table key is its id.

[channel.out]
type = "output"
color = "cyan"

[[channel.out.choices]]
label = "Phones"
device = "alsa_output.pci-0000_11_00.4.analog-stereo"
card = "alsa_card.pci-0000_11_00.4"
profile = "output:analog-stereo+input:analog-stereo"

[[channel.out.choices]]
label = "Speaker"
device = "alsa_output.pci-0000_11_00.4.iec958-stereo"
card = "alsa_card.pci-0000_11_00.4"
profile = "output:iec958-stereo+input:analog-stereo"
max_volume = 200

[[channel.out.choices]]
label = "Buds"
device = "bluez_output.*"

[channel.spotify]
type = "apps"
match = ["spotify"]
label = "Spotify"
tags = ["media"]

[channel.alice]
type = "person"
person = ["Alice", "alice-phone"]
label = "Mum"
  • Types: output, input, apps, homeassistant as today, and person (today's voip strip with person, with talking_color, max_volume, meter, launch). Two person channels with a name in common are an error, as today. auto and the group of voip strips are pools; empty is the candidate "-".
  • tags: free-form names for grouping channels, used by pools (#media) and the channels source of states. They are not conditions; name them after what the channels are (media, games), not after states.
  • Presence: whether the channel has something to control: its device is there, an application of the group has a stream, the person is in the voice chat; always for homeassistant. absent_after (default behavior.absent_after, 3 s; today's collapse_hold) delays "absent", so a reconnecting Bluetooth device does not flap; it is present again at once.
  • in_pool (default true, apps and person channels): whether the channel goes into the pools apps and auto (a person channel: people and auto) while it is present and no slot shows it (below).
  • Its Home Assistant entities are named by its id (select.xtouch_out_output), the same wherever and whenever it shows, and they stay available while it is not shown. One owner per stream: the channel, wherever it is.

Pools: one of several¶

A pool gives each slot that shows it one member that no other slot shows.

Pool Members, in this order
apps (built in) Whatever plays and has no slot: apps channels with in_pool that are present and not shown (each as a whole: its label, volume, entities, as in DAW banking), and applications that play and no channel matches. In the order they started playing. A member stays one for hold seconds after it stopped (today's auto_hold).
people (built in) The people in the voice chat, see People.
auto (built in) Both, sharing its slots; the catch-all.
[pool.<id>] channels = [...] The present channels of the list (ids, "#tag") that are not shown by a candidate of their own, in the list's order. 
[pool.auto]
color = "white"
hold = 5                     # today's behavior.auto_hold
exclude = ["speech-dispatcher", "notification"]   # never these: today's behavior.ignore

[pool.media]
channels = ["#media"]
  • The options of [pool.auto] are those of today's auto strips; a pool of channels has none (its members bring their own).
  • Presence of a pool, for if present, counts its members before slots are assigned: auto is present while anything plays that is not excluded, or anyone is in the voice chat (people take its slots; counted before the slots are assigned, so also someone whose person channel has a slot of its own); a pool of channels while one of them is present. Conditions stay independent of the result.
  • Members keep their slot in auto, so applications don't hop around (people keep their order instead). A pool of channels fills by its order instead (the first present channel gets the first slot), which is what collapse does today: Buds coming back takes its place, the others move on.
  • Select does what it does on the member (makes a device the default, launches).
  • Pick (the pick encoder of auto slots), as today: turning picks the slot's member among the applications without a slot (2/5; people are not picked); a push pins it, and it stays on the slot after it stopped playing, until the next push (pinned, auto). A pin beats the pool's order, and people don't take a pinned slot. The resolver keeps the pins: the encoder only asks for one.

People¶

Decided again on 2026-11-17, see people-slots.md: people is a built-in pool and a candidate, apps the applications alone, and auto both, sharing its slots (people first, each from its side). A pool hands a slot it has no member for to the pool or - right below it (["people", "apps"]), and a slot two pools could fill goes to the one higher in its stack. [pool.people] replaces [people] (from, order, the look), in_pool replaces in_auto, hold and exclude are options of [pool.apps]. What stays from below:

  • The members: everyone in the voice chat who has no person channel, and the person channels (with in_pool, default true) that are present and have no slot of their own, each as itself (its label, look and entities). Like a channel, a person stays a member absent_after after they left, so a reconnect moves nothing.
  • They sit in their order, for muscle memory of who sits where: order (names, or ids of person channels), then the others in the order they were first seen (kept 30 days, across restarts, in ~/.local/state/xtouch-daemon/people.json). Slots where they are the first candidate go first, then the others, each from their side: no block (see people-slots.md). Someone earlier in the order who comes moves those after them one slot on; no slot is kept empty for someone who is away.
  • People don't take a pinned slot. More people than slots wait; there is no paging. Controls of a slot (Controls) don't keep them out.
  • Every configuration has the voice chat's provider ([voip] with its defaults if the file has none), and its socket costs nothing while no voice chat connects.
  • Members are typed: an application, a channel or a person, never told apart by their names (someone called browser is no channel). People are told apart by the voice chat's id of the participant (step 4); order and the learned order go by name.

States: when something matters¶

A state is a named condition that is on or off, evaluated all the time and visible everywhere: the GUI, the live view, Home Assistant, xtouch-daemon state.

[state.call]
voice_chat = true           # in a channel of the voice chat, also alone (from the bridge)
recording = ["discord", "vesktop"]   # or: one of these records (they open a capture stream only in a call)
delay_off = 5               # stays 5 s after: a reconnect, not much longer

[state.wireless]
devices = ["bluez_output.*"]

[state.focus]
when = "not call and not gaming"

[state.night]
homeassistant = true        # Home Assistant feeds it: switch.xtouch_state_night_input
Source On while
apps One of these applications has a playback stream (playing or paused). Names and presets as match.
recording One of these applications has a capture stream. Not for Mumble: it keeps its capture stream open while connected, even alone; use voice_chat.
voice_chat The voice chat reports that you are in a channel, also alone: joining one says you mean to talk (the mic shows before anyone comes, and the people pool covers who comes).
processes A process of one of these names runs (polled every 2 s while any state uses it).
devices A device (output or input) that matches is there.
channels One of these channels is present: ids, or "#tag".
homeassistant Its input switch in Home Assistant (switch.xtouch_state_<id>_input) is on.
when The expression holds. Not together with other sources: a state is either composed or has sources.

A state with several sources is on while any of them is. Without sources it is only what its override says. delay_on (default 0) and delay_off (default behavior.state_delay_off, 3 s; for modes 10 s, today's switching.hold, which goes away; 0 for composed states, whose expression has the delays of what it names, so call and not call never overlap) apply to the sources, not to the override. References between states and modes must not form a loop; check-config names it.

Overrides¶

Every state has an override: auto (the sources decide), on or off. It is set from Home Assistant (select.xtouch_state_<id>), the GUI, the command line (xtouch-daemon state call on) or the chord. One rule for all states and modes:

  • on and off take effect at once, past the delays; back to auto, the sources decide at once too.
  • An override lasts until the sources change (an edge after their delays), then it is auto again. Example: gaming forced off while a game runs (to use the default mode for a moment); when the game exits, auto again, and the next game brings the mode.
  • persist = true keeps it across edges, until it is set to auto by hand. States without sources have no edges, so their override is kept by itself.
  • Overrides are kept across restarts. The first evaluation after a start is the baseline, not an edge: a kept override survives it.
  • An active override shows: hatched in the GUI's state bar, override in Home Assistant's attributes, and in the banner on the LCDs when it is set and when the daemon starts.

Modes¶

Modes are states with mode = true. Exactly one mode is on: the one with the highest priority whose sources are on (then the first in the file), else default. The override is one select.xtouch_mode with auto and every mode, xtouch-daemon mode gaming, mode --next/--previous, mode --auto. --next and the chord cycle through default and the modes in the order of the file (not through auto). A picked mode lasts until the rules want another mode than before; edges that don't change what the rules want (a mode of lower priority starts) and edges of modes with persist don't end it. A mode keeps label, the banner across the LCDs.

[state.gaming]
mode = true
label = "Gaming"
processes = ["@steam-games"]
apps = ["@games"]

Slots: where it shows¶

The device has 8 slots, one per physical strip, numbered 1 to 8 from the left. Each slot has a stack of candidates, top first.

[slots]
1 = "out"                   # a single candidate: the same as ["out"]
2 = "browser"
3 = ["game if gaming", "auto"]
7 = ["voip if call", "auto"]
8 = ["mic if call", "auto"]
  • A candidate is "<channel or pool>", optionally with " if <expression>", or "-": deliberately nothing (the slot is dark, today's empty). A slot whose value is a string has that one candidate.
  • A slot without an entry is "auto". A stack whose candidates all don't hold, or lose, ends in an implicit "auto"; "-" at the end makes it dark instead.
  • The table form { show = "...", when = "...", ... } takes options for this place:
  • on any candidate, what it looks like here: label, color, lit_when_idle, rec, solo, meter, meter_point;
  • on an auto candidate also what acts on an application without a channel: encoder (pick, media, volume, balance), min_volume, max_volume.
  • These apply to the applications without a channel the place shows. A channel it shows as a member (an apps channel without a slot) looks and works as the channel; the pool's look only fills in what the channel leaves open. People look as [pool.people] says. What a control does whatever the slot shows (Home Assistant, launch) is in [controls].
4 = [{ show = "auto", encoder = "pick" }]

Two words keep the rules apart: a candidate holds while its condition is true; a slot shows the candidate it gets in the end. Every candidate without a condition holds: a channel that is absent is shown dark (off) and keeps its place, an empty pool is shown dark like an auto strip without an application. "spotify if present" and "auto if present" hold only while there is something.

The resolver¶

It decides what each slot shows from the inputs (states, presence, pool members, pins, held slots) and, for pool members only, the previous result.

  1. Fixed slots. A slot that is held (a finger, a button) or within its dwell time keeps what it shows; its channel or member is reserved, and the other slots are resolved around it.
  2. Channels by rank. Every other slot proposes its first candidate that holds. A channel that several slots propose goes to the one where it is highest in the stack (its rank), ties to the leftmost slot; the others go on to their next candidate that holds. Repeat until nothing changes. A pool candidate always takes its slot in this step (pools are not contested). It ends: a slot only ever goes down its stack.
  3. Pools. The members are computed now (the auto pool needs to know which channels have a slot) and handed out: the people take the slots of auto from the right, in their order (not pinned ones, see People); pinned members go to their slots; a member of auto keeps the slot it had if that slot still shows the pool and no person took it; the others go to the remaining slots of the pool left to right, in the pool's order (a pool of channels: all by its order). Members left over wait. A slot of a pool without a member for it is dark. The pools of channels go first, then the people take their share of the slots of auto, then auto hands out its applications: a slot meant for a channel gets it before auto takes it as a whole. A pin of a channel that has a slot of its own now is dropped (one channel, one slot).

Pools never fall through and conditions never look at the result, so the steps run once each, in this order: the same inputs give the same result.

check-config warns about candidates that can never show (an earlier one always holds), channels that no stack has, launch channels that only show under conditions, and lists channels in several stacks with the slot that wins each tie.

Stability¶

  • Batching: changes are resolved together 100 ms after the first one (a game starts several streams and a process at once), so faders move once.
  • Dwell: a slot keeps what it shows for at least 1 s before it changes again.
  • Nothing changes under a finger: a held slot is fixed (step 1) until let go. A fader in touchless_faders counts as touched while the user moves it and half a second after, as today; the motor moving it to a new channel doesn't count. The buttons of the chord don't hold slots.
  • Startup: the daemon waits for the first state of the sound server and the first process scan, evaluates the states without delay_on (the baseline), restores the overrides, and only then runs the startup sequence: the first picture is the right one.
  • Reload: the previous result carries over by channel id and pool member.

Telling what changed¶

A slot that changes flashes the new label on the top line and the reason on the bottom line (call, gaming, absent) for switching.banner seconds. A mode change shows its label across all LCDs instead (no flashes per slot at the same time). The motor fader moves to the new volume, as with banking in a DAW. The daemon logs one line per resolution with the changes and the reasons: 7: auto:Spotify -> voip (call: Discord records); Spotify waits.

Controls¶

What a fader, encoder or button of a slot does whatever the slot shows: actions that belong to the place on the device, not to a channel.

[controls]
4 = { encoder = "homeassistant" }                        # number.xtouch_slot_4_encoder and push events
7 = { select = { launch = ["mumble", "mumble://..."] }, when = "not call" }
1 = { solo = "toggle:night", rec = "homeassistant" }
2 = { fader = "homeassistant", id = "desk_fader" }       # number.xtouch_desk_fader_fader
3 = [                                                    # by mode: the first whose when holds
    { encoder = "homeassistant", id = "strip_3", when = "gaming" },
    { select = { launch = ["obs"] } },
]
  • A slot's controls can claim fader, encoder (turn and push), select, rec and solo; not Mute, which stays with what the slot shows.
  • Actions: "homeassistant" (a number for the fader or encoder, events for the buttons, and a select for their LED), { launch = [...], launch_when_running = ... }, and the actions of the chord: "cycle_modes", "mode:<id>", "toggle:<state>" (the LED shows the state or mode). "toggle:<mode>" picks the mode, and the next press gives it back to the rules (auto); its LED is on while it is the mode. A fader and an encoder only take "homeassistant".
  • A slot has one table of controls or a list of them: the first whose when holds acts, the others don't (a migrated layout per entry). The entries of a slot may share an id; another slot can't have it.
  • A claimed control wins over what the slot shows and owns its LED or ring; the presenter bound there is told which are taken. With the fader taken, the encoder of what the slot shows sets the volume, as with today's fader = "homeassistant"; with only the encoder taken, the fader stays the volume. Under a claimed Select the LED is the launch's. A candidate of the slot can't set the LED (rec, solo) of a claimed button, also while the claim's when doesn't hold: one owner per LED in the file.
  • A channel's own fader/encoder = "homeassistant" (entities by channel id, moving with the channel) stays; a claim of the slot wins over it while it holds.
  • when (states only, no present): while it doesn't hold, the controls go back to what the slot shows. Starting Mumble on slot 7 outside a call leaves VoIP's own Select alone in a call. Nothing changes under a finger: a held slot keeps its claims until it is let go, and a release goes to whoever got the press.
  • Controls don't touch the resolver: they don't hold a slot, people and channels sit there as anywhere. The Solo buttons of the chord can't be claimed.
  • Their entities are *.xtouch_slot_<n>_*, or *.xtouch_<id>_* with id: registered once per configuration, always there, they follow the slot rather than what it shows.

Pitfalls the design makes possible¶

  • Launch: a channel with launch can only be launched while a slot shows it. "voip if call" hides VoIP exactly when you'd press Select to start the voice chat: put the launch into [controls] with when = "not call". check-config warns.
  • Indicators: a hidden channel's LEDs are not on the device. mic if call hides the Mic and its Rec LED, which lights while any application records (a meeting in a browser tab, OBS).
  • A voice chat without room: people in a Mumble call while no auto slot is free for them wait; the why line says so ("Bob waits").

Home Assistant¶

Entity What
The entities of a channel (number.xtouch_<id>_fader, select.xtouch_<id>_output, ...) As today's strips, named by the channel id, registered once per configuration: they stay available (and keep their state) while the channel is not shown or moves to another slot; the buttons of a homeassistant channel send events only while it is shown.
The entities of the controls of a slot (number.xtouch_slot_<n>_fader, event.xtouch_slot_<n>_buttons, ..., or <id> instead of slot_<n>) Registered once per configuration, always there, whatever the slot shows.
binary_sensor.xtouch_<id>_shown Whether the channel is on a slot.
sensor.xtouch_slot_<n> What slot n shows: a channel id (also one auto hands out), app:<application>, person:<name>, or none. Attributes label, kind (channel, app, person, none), via (the candidate: a channel or pool id), reason.
binary_sensor.xtouch_state_<id> The state. Attributes since, source (which source turned it on), override.
select.xtouch_state_<id> The override: auto, on, off.
switch.xtouch_state_<id>_input Only for homeassistant = true: what Home Assistant feeds into the state.
select.xtouch_mode The mode override: auto and every mode. Replaces select.xtouch_layout.

The chord¶

[switching] chord = { strips = [1, 8], action = "cycle_modes" } (default). action = "toggle:call" toggles the override of a state instead (auto -> the opposite of its value now -> auto). The same actions are those of buttons in [controls]; the Solo buttons of the chord can't be claimed there.

A complete example¶

The author's configuration in this format (today about 110 lines):

# [device], [behavior], [aliases], [mqtt] unchanged

[channel.out]
type = "output"
color = "cyan"
choices = [ ... ]   # the three choices above

[channel.browser]
type = "apps"
match = ["@browsers"]
label = "Browser"
color = "blue"
meter = true

[channel.game]
type = "apps"
match = ["@games"]
label = "Game"

[channel.voip]
type = "apps"
match = ["@voip"]
label = "VoIP"
color = "yellow"
lit_when_idle = true
in_pool = false             # notification sounds of Discord don't take an auto slot

[channel.mic]
type = "input"
device = "alsa_input.pci-0000_11_00.4.analog-stereo"
label = "Mic"
color = "red"
encoder = "balance"

[pool.auto]
color = "white"

[pool.people]               # they take auto slots from the right (next to VoIP), applications from the left
color = "yellow"

[state.gaming]
mode = true
label = "Gaming"
processes = ["@steam-games"]
apps = ["@games"]

[state.call]
voice_chat = true
recording = ["discord", "vesktop"]
delay_on = 0               # voice_chat and recording have no blips to swallow
delay_off = 5               # VoIP and Mic stay through a reconnect, not much longer

[slots]
1 = "out"                       # always the speaker/phones/buds
2 = "browser"
3 = ["game if gaming", "auto"]  # the game next to the browser
4 = [{ show = "auto", rec = "recording" }]
7 = ["voip if call", "auto"]
8 = ["mic if call", "auto"]

What it shows, as xtouch-daemon simulate docs/development/design/channels-states-slots.toml --table prints it (the example is that file; a test keeps this table in line with it): one row per combination of states and presence the stacks ask about that changes something.

when             1    2        3     4-6   7     8
default          out  browser  auto  auto  auto  auto
call             out  browser  auto  auto  voip  mic
gaming           out  browser  game  auto  auto  auto
gaming and call  out  browser  game  auto  voip  mic
  • People share the auto slots: they sit as a block from the right, next to VoIP, in their order (order, then the order they were first seen in); applications fill the auto slots from the left. A person takes the slot of an application only if no auto slot is free, and the application moves to a free one or waits.
  • With Mumble, joining a channel turns call on at once (delay_on = 0): VoIP, Mic and the person show up together. Leaving shows at once on the person's strip; VoIP and Mic stay 5 s, through a reconnect.
  • A candidate can set options of its strip: slot 4 sets rec (here to its default, the REC LED shows recording).

The order of the work¶

Each step keeps the daemon working and the tests green:

  1. The format: [people], no people candidate (both revised in people-slots.md), people skip pins, person channels in the block, the presence of auto, typed members, [controls] (read and checked; they run from step 5c on), talking_color only for people.
  2. Desk out of Mixer, for both formats: touch and held buttons survive a new mixer. See Step 2.
  3. Controllers get their look, identity and entity ids from outside (the old format from its strips).
  4. SlotEngine with pins, overrides and persistence, carried over a reload; the prototype takes the applications of auto from the result. Pins hold a typed member (application or channel, like Person), people go by the participant's id.
  5. The slots natively: ChannelSet, Members, Places, Controls, Binder on the Desk; Shape and the rebuilding go; entities per channel and per slot's controls.
  6. Home Assistant: states, overrides, the mode (replaces the layout select), the slots. The chord gets its new form, and its check against [controls] moves into Scene.of() (now in config/slots.py).
  7. The live view: the snapshot and requests above; the GUI shows them read-only.
  8. check-config, init-config, the docs. migrate-config was not built: the old format is a clean break, an old file fails check-config.
  9. The old format and its runtime go.
  10. The GUI edits the new format.

Step 2: the Desk¶

  • Presenter: what the Desk calls of a controller (today's StripController): strip, the on_* handlers, meter_targets, is_playing, target_gain, held_meter, close, and taken_controls (step 5).
  • The Desk API: bind(slot, presenter | None, meter_point), held_slots() (slots from 1), dispatch(event). A presenter bound to a slot a finger is on gets on_touch(True) at once, so it knows (in the old format a layout switch rebinds under a finger; in the new one a held slot doesn't change).
  • dispatch routes by slot and control (fader, encoder, each button) to an owner: the bound presenter, or a claim (a hook that stays empty until step 5 brings [controls]). It is sticky: a release (button up, encoder push up) goes to whoever got the press, even if bind() changed in between. A touched fader goes over instead: on bind() the presenter that had it gets on_touch(False), the new one on_touch(True), its moves and its release, so each sees a touch and its release.
  • Until step 9 the old format binds the controllers of its mixer through the Flow (strip_at).
  • The Desk owns the meters from step 2 on: it runs the meter loop for the presenters bound now.
  • The Desk also keeps the chord's held Solo buttons and the flashes and banners of the slots.
  • The GUI's live preview uses the same Desk: touch and held buttons carry over its start and end.
  • Tests: tests/runtime/test_desk.py with what moves from test_mixer.py, and "a finger on fader 2 during a change of slot 7 keeps slot 2 as it is, and the new presenter of slot 2 knows about the finger".

Tests the design needs¶

The resolver and the state engine are pure, so each rule gets unit tests, among them every scenario the reviews traced:

  • the example's table, row by row, and joining Mumble while gaming (VoIP, Mic and the person at once);
  • a channel several stacks want at the same and at different ranks; an exhausted stack ending in auto; "-";
  • a held slot keeping a channel another slot now ranks higher; a touchless fader moved by the user and by the motor;
  • pool members keeping their slots and waiting (6 applications, then a call); pick and pins; people in their order from the right, moving applications only when needed; a pool of channels filling by order; a hidden channel taking an auto slot as a whole, and in_pool = false;
  • batching and dwell; startup as the baseline with a kept override; a reload carrying the result over;
  • delay_on swallowing a join tone; overrides in all three values, ending at an edge, with persist;

  • composed states, loops, reserved and duplicate names, errors with positions;

Prototype¶

Three reviews of prose found three rounds of holes, each in the parts that only existed as prose. So the resolver and the state engine are code now, pure (no sound server, no device), in xtouch_daemon.slots:

Module What
model Scene.of(): reads [channel.*], [pool.*], [state.*], [slots] with every check above.
expressions The conditions, with errors at their position.
inputs, timing An Observation (applications, voice chat, processes, devices, Home Assistant), presence, the delays.
states StateEngine: sources, delays, overrides, modes, composed states, the baseline.
resolver resolve(): fixed slots, channels by rank, pools; the trace.
simulation Simulation: all of it over time, with the dwell time and held slots.
outcomes The distinct outcomes: the table above, simulate --table, the GUI's grid.
recording, replay observe records the real system; simulate --replay replays it: changes, flaps, time on.

xtouch-daemon simulate docs/development/design/channels-states-slots.toml --mode gaming --state call --playing firefox shows the slots and why. The tests (tests/slots/) have the scenarios the reviews traced that the prototype covers, and one keeps the table above in line with the example. Not in the prototype: batching (a matter of when the daemon calls it), the touch sensors (their result is held), reload carrying the result over, cycling through modes (--next), Home Assistant and the live view, the GUI, the migration.

What building it showed:

  • An override set while a delay is pending ends with it. call forced off while a join tone's delay_on runs: 2 s later the sources turn on (an edge), the override ends, and the call comes anyway. That is the rule ("until the sources change"); whether it is what people expect needs trying on the device.
  • The grid explains rank surprises. A test configuration with 1 = ["buds if present", "out"] and 3 = ["out if video", "auto"]: in video mode without buds, out goes to slot 3 (rank 1 there, 2 on slot 1), and slot 1 shows auto. Correct, but only the grid (or check-config's list of shared channels) makes it visible.
  • present on "-" meant nothing: now an error.
  • Channels of a pool without a slot don't wait: only applications and people do; such channels are just not shown.
  • A multi-line inline table is no TOML 1.0: the examples of choices use [[channel.out.choices]].
  • The code review (a DAW, Linux desktop and Home Assistant user, reviewing the code with counter-examples) found seven bugs no scenario had caught, all fixed with a test each: a held slot didn't reserve a channel it showed in auto, nor did pins check for one, so a channel could be on two slots; auto took a channel before the pool of channels meant for it; pool members had no delay (a Mumble reconnect moved a fader twice) and hold wasn't used; persist didn't apply to modes, and any mode edge ended a pick; replays missed flaps after long stable times. Also: present in a state's when is an error now, composed states have no delays of their own, simulate checks its options, and --table no longer shows composed states apart from what they name.

On the device: the daemon runs a file in this format (xtouch-daemon run -c slots.toml). The resolver runs every 0.2 s (that is the batching) on what the sound server, the processes and the voice chat of the daemon show. Each channel has its controller for as long as the configuration, each application of auto and each person without a channel one while they are members (SlotMixer in runtime/binder.py, step 5b); after each step only the slots whose result changed get another controller, so the others keep their texts, fader and LEDs; a slot that shows another candidate flashes its label and why (the when of the new candidate, or not <when> of the old one) for switching.banner seconds, unless the mode changed (its banner says it). [controls] run (runtime/controls.py, step 5c): the first entry of a slot whose when holds claims its controls on the Desk, shows their fader, ring and LEDs, and launches, picks modes, toggles states or puts them into Home Assistant. Apps channels never go into auto one application at a time, only as a whole. The mode takes the place of the layout: xtouch-daemon layout gaming, the GUI and the chord pick a mode, layout auto gives it back to the rules; the banner names a new mode. With [mqtt], Home Assistant has the entities of Home Assistant (runtime/homeassistant.py, step 6). Not yet: the GUI (it can't edit the format).

Next: see Decisions after the live test.

Decisions after the live test¶

The prototype ran on the author's device (the example above). Decided then:

  1. People only share auto (revised 2026-11-17 in people-slots.md: people, apps and auto are built-in pools). people is no candidate of a slot any more, and share goes: people always take auto slots from the right, in their order. One way to place people; no paging. Fixed people are person channels.
  2. Slots replace strips and layouts in the daemon. Not a translation into today's strips: channel controllers that live as long as the configuration, bound to slots by the resolver (The daemon).
  3. The conditions stay as they are ("x if y" strings with the grammar above): they read well and worked on the device.
  4. One format. The old one goes: no reading of it, and no migrate-config either (Migration). The GUI, the docs and init-config move to the new format.
  5. person channels without a slot join the people as themselves (in_pool to opt out), like apps channels join auto.
  6. Starting the voice chat is a control of the slot VoIP shows on: 7 = { select = { launch = [...] }, when = "not call" } in [controls]; the VoIP channel stays hidden outside a call, the strips of people have no launch.
  7. [people], not [pool.people]: people are no pool any more (revised 2026-11-17: [pool.people]).
  8. [controls]: actions that belong to a slot, whatever it shows (Controls). Options of places only look, or act on applications without a channel.

Found on the device and changed in the prototype: call on at once and off after 5 s (a delay showed VoIP and Mic after the people, a long one hid leaving); people in a learned order instead of fixed home slots (no empty slots); people from the right, applications from the left (they don't push each other around); voice_chat also alone (the mic before anyone comes); the meters of people on the dB scale like every other meter.
