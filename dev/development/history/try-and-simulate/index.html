Design: try changes and situations on the device¶

Status: built (2026-11-18), extended by the virtual twin, replaced by Previews, in the GUI or on the device (2026-11-19).

Problem¶

The GUI has two ways to see a configuration before it is real, and they don't know each other:

  • Try on the device (the live preview, live.StartPreview): the daemon runs the unsaved configuration of the editor instead of the file. The device follows every edit, but only in the situation there is right now.
  • Simulate on the page Slots: what the slots would show in another situation (another mode, states on or off, channels absent, so many people in the call, applications that play, devices without a channel). The resolver computes it in the GUI, only the drawing shows it, and nothing can be played: no daemon runs those strips.

Both answer "what would the device do if ...": with this configuration, and in this situation. Trying a situation on the device itself (the real one, or the virtual one) is what Simulate can't do.

Idea¶

One preview with two optional parts, both run by the daemon:

  • the configuration: the editor's (unsaved changes), or the file;
  • the situation: the what-if of Simulate, or the real one.

The switch "Try on the device" (next to Save and apply) sends whatever differs from reality: the unsaved changes, and while Simulate is open, its situation too. With the switch off, Simulate stays what it was before: the drawing only. So the real device never changes without that switch (decision 1).

The situation in the daemon¶

StartPreview gets an optional situation (slots.outcomes.Situation: mode, on, flipped, people, apps, devices, as gui.domain.simulate.WhatIf), and its config may be None: the file's configuration, in that situation.

The SlotEngine runs a situation in a second Simulation of its own (what_if), next to the real one:

  • States and the mode are overrides and the mode picked of that Simulation: each state of the scene is forced on or off as the situation says, the mode is picked. The real overrides and the mode picked are not touched.
  • Presence and the members come from an Observation made for the situation (Situation.observation): for a present apps channel an application its match takes, for an output/input channel its devices, for a person channel that person in the call; absent ones get none. The pools get their stand-ins: apps applications, people people, devices devices. Stand-ins are taken from what is real first (applications that play now, people in the call, devices without a channel), then made up (App 3, Person 2, Device 1): a real one has audio behind its strip, a made-up one shows its label on a strip that controls nothing.
  • Delays and dwells apply as in reality: a state switched on in the situation waits its delay_on, a channel that goes absent stays absent_after seconds. That is the point of trying it on the device.
  • The real Simulation is not stepped while a situation runs, and nothing of the situation is kept: no slots.json, no people.json.

engine.active is the Simulation that runs (the situation's, or the real one); the SlotMixer and the live view use it (see Live view for the preview around it). Home Assistant keeps the real one: its slot sensors and state sensors are not updated while a situation runs, so automations never see a made-up situation. The live view tells simulated: true in the snapshot, with the slots and states of the situation.

What the device does on its own during a situation: the chord, Home Assistant's mode select and the overrides go to the real engine (they are real requests); the device shows the situation until it ends.

The situation ends with the preview: the switch off, Simulate ended (the configuration stays previewed if it has changes), the window closed or not answering for PREVIEW_TIMEOUT, the file changed. Then the real Simulation steps again at once and the slots show reality.

The GUI¶

  • Simulate with the switch on: the what-if goes to the daemon (debounced like edits), the drawing shows the live device (with the accent frame while the situation runs), the cards show the daemon's slots and their why, and Play works: the faders of real stand-ins move their audio.
  • Simulate with the switch off: as before, the resolver in the GUI, the drawing only, no Play. Its bar says "Only in the drawing: turn on Try on the device to run it on the device."
  • The switch is there while there are unsaved changes or Simulate is open, and a daemon can take it.
  • The pill next to it: "On the device, not saved", "On the device · simulated", "On the device, not saved · simulated", "Sending to the device…", "Paused: problems".
  • Esc ends Simulate as before; with the switch on the device goes back to the real situation.

Decisions (2026-11-18)¶

  1. Simulate drives the device only while "Try on the device" is on. Without it, nothing reaches the device.
  2. One preview in the daemon (configuration and situation), not a second mechanism: the same client, heartbeat, timeout, and ending.
  3. Stand-ins are real where they can be, made up otherwise.
  4. Home Assistant never sees a situation.

Not now¶

  • The chord and Home Assistant changing the situation instead of reality while one runs.
  • A banner on the device ("SIM") while a situation runs.
