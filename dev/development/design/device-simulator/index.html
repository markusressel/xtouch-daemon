Design: a simulated device¶

Status: built (2026-11-17), extended 2026-11-18 (see Decisions).

Why¶

The X-Touch Extender used for development was defective (the touch sensors of faders 1 and 4 failed) and went back. Without a device nothing above the protocol can be tried outside the tests: no slots on strips, no fader under a finger while a slot changes, no LEDs and texts to look at. The protocol and the behavior of the device are known well (see Hardware, verified on the device on 2026-11-15), so a simulated device can stand in for it: for development, for the GUI, and for tests that run the real Surface end to end.

The model is also how the GUI sees the device (decided 2026-11-17): the daemon keeps one as a mirror of the device it drives, and the GUI shows the mirror. One model of the device, then, for the virtual device, the GUI and the tests.

Goals¶

  • The daemon talks to the simulated device with the same bytes as to the real one. Everything above the transport (Surface, its touch grace, touchless faders, the glide of the motors, the startup sequence, reconnects) runs unchanged, so it gets tried too.
  • The simulated device behaves like the real one where Hardware says how, including faults: a touch sensor that fails (like the returned unit), the device switched off and on (faders move by themselves), unplugged.
  • One model of the device, without IO and without Qt, behind its frontends: the mirror in the daemon (what the GUI shows), a socket server for the daemon, the GUI's drawing to use it, and a test fixture.
  • No root, no kernel modules: it has to work in CI and in a user session.

Not a goal: proving timing-sensitive motor behavior (slow moves, the stop short of the bottom, motor noise). The simulator models the motor simply; those need the real device.

Rejected: a virtual ALSA MIDI device¶

The kernel's snd-virmidi makes raw MIDI devices that Surface would find in /proc/asound/cards without any change. But it needs modprobe (root), its other end is the ALSA sequencer (a second API for the simulator), and it doesn't work in CI. A transport of our own is simpler and testable.

Design¶

1. A transport seam in Surface¶

  • hardware/transport.py: a Transport protocol with what Surface uses of RawMidi (write, drain, close, is_open; data and the end come through the callbacks on_data and on_closed). RawMidi is one.
  • A Port finds and opens a transport, LookupError when there is no device:
  • AlsaPort(device_name, proc_asound_cards, dev_snd): finds the card in /proc/asound/cards and opens /dev/snd/midiC<card>D0 as RawMidi.
  • SocketPort(path): connects to the simulator's Unix socket. No simulator running is "not connected", so Surface.run() waits and connects when it starts, and a closed simulator is an unplugged device: the reconnect path gets used.
  • Surface(port=...); the daemon picks the port from the configuration: [device] virtual = true (or xtouch-daemon run --virtual-device, which wins) uses SocketPort(instance.virtual_device_socket_file()), next to the other runtime files ($XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/xtouch-daemon/).
  • The log says which: "Connected to the simulated X-Touch-Ext at ...".

2. The messages to the device, decoded¶

hardware/protocol.py builds the messages to the device (fader, led, ring, meter, lcd_text, lcd_colors) and decodes those from it. The simulator needs the other direction:

  • decode_host(message) -> HostEvent | None with MotorMoved(strip, value), LedSet(strip, button, Led), RingSet(strip, position, mode, center), MeterSet(strip, level), LcdText(offset, text), LcdColors(colors).
  • And the encoders for what the device sends: fader_moved, fader_touched, button, encoder_turned, encoder_pressed, host_query.
  • Round-trip tests: every encoder against its decoder, both directions, over the full value ranges.

3. The model: simulator/device.py¶

SimulatedDevice, pure (no IO, no asyncio, no Qt): it takes bytes from the host and the time, and gives bytes for the host. Time comes in as an argument (now), so tests and frontends drive it.

State per strip:

  • LCD: two lines of 7 characters (written by offset into the 2 x 56 characters, as the device does), and the color.
  • The LEDs of Rec, Solo, Mute and Select: off, on, flashing.
  • The ring: position 0..11, mode, center LED.
  • The meter: the level and when it was set; it falls by itself. The rate of the fall is not measured: about one level per 150 ms, marked as an assumption (see "Fidelity").
  • The fader: where it is (14 bits, but positions in multiples of 4 as the device reports them), the motor's target, touched or not. The motor goes to the target at a fixed speed (about 100 ms over the whole range), not while touched; a motor move sends nothing back (the real device doesn't either).
  • The encoder: pressed or not.

Input (what a hand does), each giving the bytes the device sends:

  • touch(strip, touched), move(strip, fraction): a move while not touched follows the real device: with touch_delay (default 0.07 s) the touch comes after the first moves, as with a quick hand.
  • press(strip, button, pressed), turn(strip, steps), push(strip, pressed).

Faults and events:

  • broken_touch: set[int]: these faders never report a touch (the returned unit: {0, 3}).
  • power_cycle(): sends the host query, then the faders go down by themselves (moves without a touch), as after switching the device on; texts, colors and LEDs are gone until the host sends them again.
  • The host query on connect: F0 00 00 66 15 01 <serial> <challenge> F7 with a made-up serial.
  • Unplugging is the frontends' business (they close the connection).

A changed callback (or a version counter) tells frontends to repaint.

4. The mirror: what the GUI shows¶

The Surface keeps a SimulatedDevice as the mirror of the device: every byte it sends goes into it (also while the device is away: then it is what the device will show once it is there), and what the device sends goes in too (observe(): fader moves, touches, encoder pushes). The device's query after switching on changes nothing: the Surface shows everything again on each connect, and after one the mirror doesn't know where the faders are until the motor moved them or the device reported a move (forget_faders()). The live view's strips are the mirror's state (strip_states()), so the GUI shows what reached the device, banners and flashes included, with the motor and the meters moving as the model moves them. The Surface keeps its own record of what each strip shows only where it needs it to send (the strips' values and looks).

What the mirror can't know: what the device does without saying (a motor that stopped short, where the faders are after a connect). It shows what was sent and what the device reported.

5. The virtual device and its frontends¶

simulator/server.py: a Unix socket server at instance.virtual_device_socket_file() with one SimulatedDevice. One client at a time (the daemon); a second one is refused, like a device another program has open. A tick (every 10 ms) moves the motors and lets the meters fall. The same device stays when the daemon disconnects and connects again, so a daemon restart sees the faders where they were, as on the real device.

  • CLI: xtouch-daemon virtual-device runs the server headless, logs what the host sets at debug level, and takes a few options (--broken-touch 1,4). (Not simulate: that one simulates slots.)
  • GUI: the GUI can host the server itself (a QLocalServer around the same SimulatedDevice). Then its drawing of the device takes the mouse, sent to the hosted device as a hand:
  • a fader: press = touch, drag = moves, release = let go; Alt-drag moves it without a touch;
  • the buttons: click (press and release), held while the mouse is down;
  • the encoder: the wheel or a drag on the knob turns it (up or right is clockwise), a click pushes it, holding holds it down, and holding on an LED of the ring turns it until the ring lights that LED (gui/qt/preview/encoder_hand.py);
  • a toolbar: plug in / unplug, switch off and on, broken touch per fader.

What it paints still comes from the live view (the daemon's mirror), which is the hosted device's state once it got there; the GUI doesn't paint two models. While the daemon waits for a device that isn't connected, the pill "X-Touch-Ext not connected" offers "Open the virtual device"; nothing starts without a click. - Tests: a fixture that connects a real Surface to a SimulatedDevice through an in-memory transport (no socket), with the loop's clock (looptime). New end-to-end tests: touch grace, touchless faders, the glide arriving, the startup sequence, a power cycle, a reconnect, a slot change under a finger. RecordingSurface stays for the tests that only look at what was sent.

6. Fidelity¶

What the simulator does is either verified on the device (it says so in Hardware) or an assumption; the assumptions are listed in the module's docstring and in the docs: the speed of the motor, the fall of the meters, the delay of the touch, the moves after switching on. The interactive test is meant to replace assumptions with comparisons on the device.

Decisions¶

  • (2026-11-17) The CLI command is xtouch-daemon virtual-device, the option [device] virtual = true, and xtouch-daemon run --virtual-device overrides it.
  • (2026-11-17) The GUI offers the virtual device but doesn't start it: while the daemon waits for a device that isn't connected, the pill "X-Touch-Ext not connected" has "Open the virtual device". Nothing starts without a click.
  • (2026-11-18) The mouse on the drawing is a hand only in Play; Select (the default) picks and moves slots. Play also works on the device of a running daemon through the live view (live.Play, Surface.play()), so the GUI is a remote control for the real device too. While no daemon runs, "Try without a device" starts one with the virtual device on the file and hosts it: a demo for people without the device. It is the one thing the GUI starts by itself, and only on that click; it ends with the window.
