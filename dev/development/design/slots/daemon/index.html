In the daemon¶

Part of Design: channels, states and slots. The modules are in Architecture.

The runtime¶

Slots replace the strips and layouts of the old format in the daemon natively (no translation into strips): parts that live as long as the daemon, the configuration or a member, and a binder that changes only the slots whose result changed.

Part Lives as long as What
Desk (daemon/desk.py) the daemon The 8 strips of the device: touches, held buttons and touchless faders (held_slots()), the events to the presenter bound to each slot or to a claim, the meters of what is bound, banners and flashes.
SlotEngine (daemon/slots.py) the daemon The StateEngine, presence, the members of the pools, the resolver with dwell, pins and the order of the people; keeps overrides, the mode picked and the people across restarts; wakes at the deadlines of the delays (no polling). A reload carries its state over by state, channel and member id.
SlotMixer (daemon/binder.py) the configuration A controller with its own VirtualStrip per channel, per application of auto and per person without a channel while they are members (made when it becomes one, closed when its hold or absent_after ends; it moves between slots unchanged). Binds only the slots whose result changed; a controller gets its look and the options of its place when it is bound, its identity is its channel or member.
Controls (daemon/controls.py) the configuration The [controls] of each slot with their entities, bound to the Desk's slot, not to what it shows.
SlotEntities (daemon/homeassistant.py) the configuration The entities of the states, overrides, the mode and the slots.
  • Times come from the loop's clock (loop.time()).
  • The Desk routes by slot and control (fader, encoder, each button) to an owner: the bound presenter or a claim. It is sticky: a release goes to whoever got the press, even if a bind() came in between. A touched fader goes over instead: on bind() the presenter that had it gets on_touch(False), the new one on_touch(True), its moves and its release, so each sees a touch and its release. A live preview uses the same Desk, so touches and held buttons carry over its start and end.

Home Assistant¶

Entity What
The entities of a channel (number.xtouch_<id>_fader, select.xtouch_<id>_output, ...) Named by the channel id, registered once per configuration: they stay available (and keep their state) while the channel is not shown or moves; the buttons of a homeassistant channel send events only while it is shown.
The entities of the controls of a slot (number.xtouch_slot_<n>_fader, event.xtouch_slot_<n>_buttons, ..., or <id> instead of slot_<n>) Registered once per configuration, whatever the slot shows.
binary_sensor.xtouch_<id>_shown Whether the channel is on a slot.
sensor.xtouch_slot_<n> What slot n shows: a channel id, app:<application>, person:<name>, or none. Attributes label, kind (channel, app, person, none), via (the candidate), reason.
binary_sensor.xtouch_state_<id> The state. Attributes since, source (which source turned it on), override.
select.xtouch_state_<id> The override: auto, on, off.
switch.xtouch_state_<id>_input Only with homeassistant = true: what Home Assistant feeds into the state.
select.xtouch_mode The mode override: auto and every mode.

Commands¶

  • check-config prints the channels, the outcome table and its notes (see Slots).
  • list-apps says per application which channel, pool or slot has it.
  • why <slot or channel> tells what a slot shows and why not the candidates above it, or where a channel is and why not elsewhere (browser: on slot 2; not slot 8 (on slot 2 (tie, to the left))); why people who sits where and who waits.
  • simulate and observe: the slots of a file in any situation, and a recording of the real system to replay (simulate --replay).

The GUI¶

  • Slots: each slot is a card with its stack, a row per candidate with its condition as text chips (call or gaming, always) and a tiny LCD in the color of its strip, ▶ on the one shown. Drag and drop or Ctrl+Up/Down reorder. The inspector edits the candidate (condition, the options for this place), says where else its channel is used and what a pool hands out and from where; below it the slot's controls. The why line is the resolver's trace.
  • Simulate: what-if inputs (every state, the presence the stacks ask about, how many people are in the call and applications play); the drawing shows what the resolver would, in the GUI or on the device where "Preview" says, see Previews. Esc ends it.
  • The state bar is live: each state with its value and since when; its menu sets the override, and an overridden state is hatched, with ⟲ back to auto.
  • Channels: channels and pools (the built-in ones below, each with the look of its strips), with type, label, tags and where they are used; Add, Duplicate, Remove.
  • States: modes first, then the other states: sources or when, delays, persist, the live value since when and from which source, the override, and which stacks use it.
  • Problems link to a slot, candidate, channel, pool or state. The GUI keeps the form a stack has in the file (string, compact list or tables) and its comments.
