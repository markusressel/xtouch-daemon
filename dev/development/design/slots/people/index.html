Where the people sit¶

Part of Design: channels, states and slots. Status: built (2026-11-17), the third round of the design. Its rules are in Pools and the resolver.

Why¶

At first the people of the voice chat had no candidate of their own: the resolver put them on the slots of auto from the right, while applications filled them from the left. That fits one layout (the voice chat's strips on the right), and the file could say nothing else: not the voice chat on the left, not people in the middle, not people only in a call, not "a call replaces the applications". The GUI didn't show that auto takes people at all.

So there are three built-in pools, each a candidate like any channel: apps (the applications), people (the people) and auto (both, sharing its slots, the people first). A pool hands a slot it has no member for to the pool or - right below it, so ["people", "apps"] is a person, else an application.

Not a block¶

People inside auto (and in people) are no block: they take the slots where nothing is above them first, then the others, each group from their side. In the live test's file with call overridden off the order was 6, 5, 4, then 8, 7, 3, 2. This stays, for a reason: people keep no slot (they sit in their order, recomputed every step), so a slot that a candidate above can take back (voip if call) would move every one of them when it does. Applications keep the slot they had, so for them the side alone decides (as before: slot 2 before slot 3, whatever is above them).

Use cases¶

A, B, ... are people in their order; "call" is the state call (Mumble: voice_chat = true). The tables come from the resolver (without a previous result).

U1: voice chat on the right (Markus)¶

[pool.people]
color = "yellow"

[pool.apps]
color = "white"

[slots]
1 = "main"
2 = ["browser if present", "apps", "people"]
3 = ["game if present", "apps", "people"]
4 = ["people", { show = "apps", rec = "recording" }]
5 = ["people", "apps"]
6 = ["people", "apps"]
7 = ["voip if call", "apps"]
8 = ["mic if call", "apps"]
Situation 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 Waits
No call, nothing plays people people apps apps apps apps apps
No call, browser and Spotify play browser Spotify apps apps apps apps apps
Call, 1 person people people apps apps A voip mic
Call, 3 people people people C B A voip mic
Call, 3 people, browser and Spotify play browser Spotify C B A voip mic
Call, 7 people E D C B A voip mic F, G
Call, 7 people, browser and Spotify play browser Spotify C B A voip mic D, E, F, G

On 2 and 3 applications come before people: in a crowded call a playing application still gets a fader. A dark slot shows the last pool of its chain (people on 2 and 3).

Applications fill from the right, so slot 2 comes last; from is a side, not "the edges first".

U4: people only in a call¶

Matters when call means less than "someone is in the voice chat", e.g. sitting in a Mumble lobby all evening with call toggled by a button. Slots that should never show people say apps: auto, also the implicit one at the end of a stack that can run out, takes people.

[slots]
1 = "main"
2 = "apps"
3 = "apps"
4 = ["people if call", "apps"]
5 = ["people if call", "apps"]
6 = ["people if call", "apps"]
7 = ["voip if call", "apps"]
8 = ["mic if call", "apps"]

[controls]
8 = { solo = "toggle:call" }
Situation 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 Waits
3 people in the lobby, call off, Spotify plays Spotify apps apps apps apps apps apps A, B, C
call on, 3 people, Spotify plays Spotify apps C B A voip mic
call on, 3 people, Spotify and mpv play Spotify mpv C B A voip mic

U6: the simple case¶

No entry, or auto: people from the right, applications from the left, on every slot that shows auto, as before.

Not chosen¶

  • People only as a pool, pools never fall through: slots meant for people are dark outside a call (the live test rejected that).
  • auto alone with options in [people] (from, slots, when, alone): a second language next to the stacks.
  • A preference per auto place (people = "first"): the same as stacks, hidden in an option.
  • A list of slots for people ([people] slots = [6, 5, 4]): a second place to edit when a slot moves.
  • A real block, or "first candidate first" for applications too: see Not a block.
  • +2 or paging when more people than slots: they wait; the why line and the live view say who.
