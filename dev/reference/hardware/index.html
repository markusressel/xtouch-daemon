Hardware and protocol¶

The Behringer X-Touch Extender has 8 strips. Each has a 100 mm motorized, touch-sensitive fader, four buttons with LEDs (Rec, Solo, Mute, Select), an encoder with a push button and an LED ring, an LED meter, and an LCD with two lines of 7 characters and one of 8 backlight colors.

The meter has 8 LEDs: seven for the level (green, then orange) and a red clip LED at the top. Mackie Control sets it in 14 levels, about two per LED (only the 14th lights the clip LED), and the device lets it fall by itself. What the levels mean on an audio strip, and whether they are taken before or after the fader, is in Meters.

xtouch-daemon drives it over USB in its Mackie Control mode, so it acts as the "host" a DAW would be. The device needs no driver: it is a USB class compliant MIDI device.

First steps sets the device up (MC mode on USB) and tests it.

Linux access¶

Linux lists the extender as a sound card (/proc/asound/cards: XTouchExt ... X-Touch-Ext) with the raw MIDI device /dev/snd/midiC<card>D0. xtouch-daemon finds the card by its name ([device] name) and opens that device directly, without ALSA sequencer or JACK.

The user needs read and write access to it. Desktop sessions give it to the logged-in user through the seat (logind). Without a session, add the user to the audio group.

Only one program can have the device open: close DAWs that use it. xtouch-daemon test asks a running daemon to test the device.

To check a device: xtouch-daemon test, or "Test the device" in the GUI, which draws the device next to it. Every strip answers its controls, so each LED, the ring in each mode, the LCD colors, the meter, the motor and the touch sensor of every fader can be checked by hand, and compared with the drawing. The modes with +c set the center bit of the ring's message, which lights the two LEDs at the ends of the ring (see the table below).

Motor faders¶

  • Touch: while a finger is on a fader, its motor does not follow outside changes. When the fader is let go, it moves to the strip's current value, usually the one that was set last.
  • Only a touched fader sets a value: the device reports a touch about 50-100 ms after the first moves of a quick hand. A move without a touch waits for one for 0.3 s with the motor still: with a touch it was the hand and counts, without one it was the device itself (e.g. its faders going down after it was switched on), and the fader goes back to its value. A fader whose touch sensor failed can't change the volume until it is repaired, or until it is one of the touchless_faders. With touch_required = false, every move of every fader counts.
  • Smooth motion: a fader glides to a new value instead of jumping there. It follows its target like a critically damped spring (no overshoot) that gets there in about 100 ms, starting from where it is, also from where a hand left it. The spring keeps its speed when the target moves on, so a volume dragged in another mixer, which arrives in small steps, becomes one even movement.
  • Arriving: once a fader is there, its value is sent twice more (after 0.15 s and 0.4 s), in case the motor stopped short of it. Stopping the daemon waits for that.
  • Slow moves: the big moves glide three times slower, and stay slow until the fader arrived: a fader that is put away because its strip goes dark (the daemon stops, an application ended, a reload), and the first move after a connect. After a fast move the motor stops a little short of the bottom; a slow one gets there. Tried on the device: neither sending the value again nor a nudge (a little up, then down again) did as well.
  • Startup and shutdown sequences: after a connect the strips light up and their faders come up; when the daemon stops they go dark and their faders go down. By default one after the other, from the left and from the right; [device] startup and shutdown choose others, see Startup and shutdown sequences. The device can't blink a ring and has no brightness levels, so the light effects are steps of on and off. In show each fader moves only twice: up with the light and down with it (at startup to its value, at shutdown to the bottom), smoothly.
  • Disconnect: a device that goes away (unplugged or switched off) gets no shutdown sequence: nothing reaches it any more. Switched off, its faders stay where they are; switched on again, the device moves them to the bottom by itself, and after the connect the daemon brings them up to their values. Whether the device moves them, or keeps its LCDs and LEDs, when only the USB cable is pulled while it stays on is not known yet; the daemon assumes them at the bottom after every connect either way (see Start).
  • Start: the device can't tell where its faders are. It reports a position only while a finger moves a fader, not after a connect, not on the device query, not after a motor move. So after a connect a fader is assumed at the bottom, where the daemon leaves the faders when it stops, and glides from there. If it was moved while the daemon did not run, its first step jumps from where it really is.
  • Motor noise: slight steps can be heard while a fader moves, more above about 70 %. They come from the device, not from the values sent: a perfectly even 14-bit ramp at 100 Hz sounds the same. The motor control works in small steps (the positions the device reports are multiples of 4, so 12 bits), and the slider has play on its rail: the belt tilts it one way going up and the other way going down, which clicks. Don't grease it: the touch sensing goes through the knob and slider.
  • Fast moves: the device sends many positions per second. Only the latest one is set when the previous command finished, so a slow sound server does not build a queue.

The virtual device¶

xtouch-daemon virtual-device and the GUI simulate the device (simulator/device.py), byte for byte, on a Unix socket in the runtime directory instead of /dev/snd. The daemon keeps the same model as a mirror of the real device, which is what the GUI draws. What it does like the device, as described on this page: the messages of Protocol both ways, the LCD as two lines of characters across the strips, a touch on a fader, positions reported in steps of 4, the motor that moves only when the host sets a value or a finger lets go, never under a finger, a fader whose touch sensor failed (no touch reported), and switching it off and on (its query, everything dark, the faders going down by themselves and reporting it).

What is assumed: how fast the motor goes (0.1 s over the whole travel), how fast a meter falls (one level per 0.15 s), and when the touch of a quick hand comes (70 ms after its first move).

Protocol¶

The Mackie Control protocol; the extender is an MCU XT (model id 0x15). Verified on the device (2026-11-15) unless marked otherwise. n is the strip, 0..7.

From the device¶

Element Message
Host query F0 00 00 66 15 01 <7 byte serial> <4 byte challenge> F7, sent on its own after start. The device works without an answer.
Fader Pitch bend on channel n: En lsb msb, 14 bits (0x0000..0x3FFF)
Fader touch Note 0x68 + n, velocity 7F touched, 00 released
Buttons Note on, velocity 7F pressed, 00 released: Rec 0x00 + n, Solo 0x08 + n, Mute 0x10 + n, Select 0x18 + n
Encoder turn CC 0x10 + n: 01..3F clockwise, 41..7F counter-clockwise (the low 6 bits are the steps)
Encoder push Note 0x20 + n

To the device¶

Element Message
Fader motor Pitch bend, like the fader position
Button LEDs Note of the button, velocity 7F on, 01 flashing, 00 off
LED ring CC 0x30 + n, value 0x40 (center LED) | mode << 4 (0 dot, 1 boost/cut, 2 wrap, 3 spread) | position 0 (off) or 1..11. The ring has 13 LEDs: the positions light the middle 11, and Mackie Control's center LED (0x40) lights the two at the ends, together.
Meter Channel pressure D0, value n << 4 \| level, level 0..14. One more of the 8 LEDs every two levels (1–2 light one, 9–10 five), 11 lights six, 12–13 seven, 14 all eight with the red one at the top. 15 lights none. The device lets every level fall by itself (14 too: there is no overload LED it holds, unlike Mackie Control's 0x0E), so it must be sent again to hold a level.
LCD text F0 00 00 66 15 12 <offset> <ASCII> F7, offset line * 56 + n * 7
LCD colors F0 00 00 66 15 72 <8 colors> F7 (a Behringer extension, works in MC mode): 0 black, 1 red, 2 green, 3 yellow, 4 blue, 5 magenta, 6 cyan, 7 white

The codec is in hardware/protocol.py: pure functions that decode the device's messages into events and build the messages that drive it.

Messages the daemon does not use are not lost: xtouch-daemon test prints them (unknown message: ...), and the daemon logs them at debug level (xtouch-daemon run -l debug). They show what a device sends that differs from the table above, e.g. a touch that is not the usual note.

See Troubleshooting for problems with the device.
