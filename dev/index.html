xtouch-daemon¶

xtouch-daemon turns a Behringer X-Touch Extender into a hardware mixer for PipeWire on Linux. Its motor faders, LED meters and scribble strips control your devices and applications. A strip can also belong to Home Assistant instead, for lights, speakers in other rooms or scenes.

Features¶

Strips¶

  • Device strips (output, input) control an output or an input, or whatever is the default. Select makes the device the default, and its LED shows which one is. See Devices.
  • Several devices on one strip: a list or a * wildcard (e.g. bluez_output.* for any Bluetooth headphones). The strip shows the one that is there, and Select switches between them.
  • Sound card profiles: Rec switches the card between profiles, e.g. analog and digital output. The label of the active profile names the strip, and each profile can have its own volume range.
  • Application strips (apps) control all streams of a group of applications at once, e.g. every browser. A stream that starts later gets the strip's volume. See Applications.
  • Auto strips (auto) pick up the next application that plays and has no strip of its own. They keep it for a few seconds after it stops, so it does not jump between strips from track to track. The encoder picks another application, a push pins it. Aliases give applications readable names.
  • Home Assistant strips (homeassistant) turn a strip into entities for your automations over MQTT discovery: the motor fader, the encoder, the LCD lines and color, the LEDs, and events for every button press. See Home Assistant.
  • Empty strips (empty) stay dark and do nothing.

On every strip¶

  • Motor faders glide to new values smoothly, follow changes made anywhere else, and stay still while your finger is on them. Only a touched fader changes a value.
  • Volume ranges: the fader covers min_volume to max_volume, up to 200 % (amplified in software). See Strips.
  • Scribble strips show a label and the volume (or muted, idle, off) on a backlight in the strip's color. A strip whose device or applications are not there goes dark.
  • Mute button with LED.
  • Encoder modes: the encoder sets the balance, fine-tunes the volume, picks the application of an auto strip, or seeks and plays/pauses the media player of the applications. Its LED ring shows what it controls.
  • Home Assistant controls on audio strips: the encoder (or the fader) becomes an entity in Home Assistant, e.g. to dim a light, while the other control keeps the volume. See Encoders.
  • Rec and Solo LEDs as indicators: recording, playing, default or muted. See LEDs.
  • LED meters show the level of each strip, before or after its fader (meter_point), and light their red LED for half a second when the audio clips (a peak at full scale). They are passive: they never wake a device, never light your desktop's microphone indicator, and never switch Bluetooth headphones into headset mode.

Around it¶

  • GUI editor (xtouch-daemon gui): the eight strips as the device shows them right now, editing with errors next to the options, suggestions from the sound server, drag to reorder, undo, a live preview that runs the unsaved changes on the device, and save-and-apply that keeps the comments of the file. See GUI.
  • Command line tools: a device self-test, lists of devices, profiles and applications, a configuration check. See Commands.
  • Robust: survives unplugging or switching off the device and restarts of the sound server and the MQTT broker, and shows everything again when they are back.
  • A proper service: a systemd user service with readiness notification (Type=notify). systemctl --user reload applies a changed configuration without a restart, and the log goes to the journal with levels. Startup and shutdown sequences light the strips up and turn them dark: one after the other, all at once, like a curtain or behind a wave of light. When it stops, it leaves the surface dark.

Requirements¶

  • Linux with PipeWire and pipewire-pulse (or PulseAudio).
  • pw-record (part of PipeWire) for the meters.
  • Python 3.14.
  • A Behringer X-Touch Extender in MC mode on USB (see Getting started).
  • For Home Assistant strips: an MQTT broker that Home Assistant uses (e.g. the Mosquitto add-on).
