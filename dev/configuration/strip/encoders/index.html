Encoders¶

The fader of an audio strip sets the volume, unless it is in Home Assistant or bound to a value of your own. The encoder does what the strip's encoder option says, and its LED ring shows it. After a turn or a push, the second line of the LCD shows the exact value for 2 seconds instead of the volume; moving the fader ends that.

encoder Turn Push Ring LCD
volume Changes the volume by behavior.encoder_step per step; the fader follows. Sets behavior.reset_volume. The volume, lit from the left. The volume.
balance Moves the balance between left and right, 5 % per step. Nothing for mono devices and streams. Centers it. From the center to the side. L 15%, center, R 5%.
pick Picks the application among those without a strip of their own. Pins it. Which of them, as a dot; pinned, lit up to there. Which of how many (2/5); pinned or auto.
media Seeks in the track of the applications' media player, 5 s per step. Plays or pauses. How far the track is, lit from the left; blinking while paused, dark while stopped. Where the track is (1:05); playing or paused.
homeassistant Changes a number entity in Home Assistant. An event in Home Assistant. The number. The number (42%).
{ set, watch, ... } Changes a value of your own by its step. Sets its push. The value, lit from the left. The value, in its format.

Which channel types have which modes, the default first:

Channel type Encoder modes
output, input balance, volume, homeassistant
apps media, volume, balance, homeassistant
apps, auto (a place in a slot, or the pool) pick, media, volume, balance
devices (a place in a slot, or the pool) balance, volume

The strips of people have no encoder. On a slot, whatever it shows, [controls] can give the encoder to Home Assistant: 5 = { encoder = "homeassistant" } (see Controls), or to the value of a custom channel, e.g. the brightness of a screen: 5 = { encoder = { channel = "screen" } }.

A mode that the type does not have is an error, e.g. channel.web: encoder: pick is not for apps strips (media, volume, balance, homeassistant). With fader = "homeassistant", or a fader bound to a value, the default is volume, and it is the only mode. xtouch-daemon check-config shows the encoder of every channel.

homeassistant channels have no encoder option: their encoder is a Home Assistant entity, see Home Assistant.

[channel.out]
type = "output"
label = "Speaker"
encoder = "volume"   # fine steps instead of the balance

[slots]
5 = [{ show = "auto", encoder = "media" }]   # pick is the default for auto

volume¶

Fine steps of the volume. One step is behavior.encoder_step percent (1 by default), and the fader follows. A push sets behavior.reset_volume (100 % by default). Both stay within the volume range of the channel.

balance¶

The balance between the left and the right channel. One step moves it 5 %, so 20 steps go from the center to one side. A push centers it. The ring lights from its center towards the side the balance leans to; only a balance exactly in the center lights the center LED alone, so one a hair off lights the next LED too. The LCD shows the exact balance, e.g. L 15% (the left side 15 % towards the full left), R <1% or center. On mono devices and streams the encoder does nothing.

pick¶

For auto. Turning the encoder picks the slot's application among those without a channel of their own, paused ones too. One that another auto slot has swaps with it. It works on a slot without an application too.

A push pins the application: it stays on the slot after it stopped playing, until the next push. The ring shows which of the candidates the slot has, as a dot; while pinned, it is lit up to there. The LCD shows which one of how many (2/5) after a turn, and pinned or auto (picked by itself again) after a push.

media¶

The default for apps channels. The encoder controls the media player of the strip's applications: Spotify, a browser, mpv with mpv-mpris, anything that speaks MPRIS on the session bus.

media
Turn Seeks 5 seconds per step.
Push Plays or pauses.
Ring Fills up from the left with how far the track is, also while the strip is idle (e.g. paused). It blinks (0.5 s on, 0.5 s off) while the track is paused and is dark while it is stopped, or for live streams without a length.
LCD Where the track is after a seek (1:05), and what a push made of it (playing, paused).

The player is the one of the process that plays a stream of the strip or of one of its parents, since browsers play from a child process. Without such a player, one named like the application (spotify for Spotify). Playing streams come first. Applications without a player leave the encoder without a function.

A paused player keeps the strip active, also after its streams are gone: browsers end the stream of a paused video a few seconds after the pause, and the strip would go dark with it. It stays lit with its fader at the volume, and the ring blinks; a move of the fader sets the volume the stream gets when it plays again. The strip goes dark when the player is stopped or closed. On a slot of apps or auto this lasts as long as the slot keeps the application (hold of [pool.apps]).

Needs the session bus

xtouch-daemon needs the D-Bus session bus for this, which a systemd user service has. Without it the daemon logs No media players: the session bus is not reachable (...) once and keeps trying.

homeassistant¶

The encoder belongs to Home Assistant, the fader still sets the volume. For example, the encoder dims the light next to the speakers. Turning changes number.<ns>_<id>_encoder (0..100) by behavior.encoder_step per step, the ring shows it, the LCD shows the value (42%), and a push sends encoder_pressed/encoder_released on event.<ns>_<id>_buttons. It works while the strip is idle too. An automation in Home Assistant decides what the number does, see Home Assistant.

[channel.desk]
type = "output"
device = "alsa_output.usb-*"  # number.xtouch_desk_encoder
encoder = "homeassistant"

A value of your own¶

The encoder sets a value that programs of your own set and watch, e.g. the brightness of a screen with system-control (sysctrl). The fader still sets the volume. The options are those of a value in Bindings (set, watch, min, max, step, push, format), and they are explained in Custom channels.

The encoder can also set an entity of Home Assistant, e.g. encoder = { entity = "media_player.kitchen" } for the volume of a speaker. See An entity of Home Assistant.

[channel.out]
type = "output"
label = "Speaker"
encoder = { set = "sysctrl display redshift brightness -d DisplayPort-1 -b {value}", watch = "sysctrl display redshift brightness -d DisplayPort-1 --watch", min = 0.1, max = 1.0, push = 1.0, format = "{value:.0%}" }

fader = "homeassistant"¶

The other way round: the fader belongs to Home Assistant (number.<ns>_<id>_fader, with fader_touched and fader_released events), and the encoder sets the volume in fine steps. The LCD still shows the volume. A fader bound to a value of your own does the same: the encoder sets the volume. Both need the [mqtt] section.

[channel.music]
type = "apps"
match = ["spotify"]
label = "Music"
fader = "homeassistant"      # number.xtouch_music_fader; encoder: volume
