Slots¶

Part of Design: channels, states and slots.

The device has 8 slots, one per strip, numbered 1 to 8 from the left. Each has a stack of candidates, top first.

[slots]
1 = "out"                   # a single candidate: the same as ["out"]
2 = "browser"
3 = ["game if gaming", "auto"]
4 = [{ show = "auto", encoder = "pick" }]
7 = ["voip if call", "auto"]
8 = ["mic if call", "auto"]
  • A candidate is "<channel or pool>", optionally with " if <expression>", or "-": deliberately nothing (the slot is dark).
  • A slot without an entry is "auto". A stack whose candidates all don't hold, or lose, ends in an implicit "auto"; "-" at the end makes it dark instead.
  • Two words keep the rules apart: a candidate holds while its condition is true; a slot shows the candidate it gets in the end. A candidate without a condition always holds: an absent channel is shown dark and keeps its place, an empty pool hands down or is dark. "spotify if present" holds only while there is something.
  • Options for this place, in the table form { show = "...", when = "...", ... }:
  • on a channel, its look here: label, color, lit_when_idle, rec, solo, meter, meter_point; on a pool the same without label (its strips show the names of their members);
  • on apps and auto also what acts on an application without a channel: encoder (pick, media, volume, balance), min_volume, max_volume; on devices encoder (balance, volume), min_volume, max_volume, select; on people only color, meter and meter_point.
  • The built-in pools ([pool.apps], ...) take the same options as their places. A Home Assistant encoder or fader, launch and an id are controls of the slot, in [controls].
  • They apply to the members without a channel. A channel shown as a member looks and works as the channel; the place only fills in what it leaves open. What a control does whatever the slot shows is in [controls].

The resolver¶

slots.resolver.resolve() decides what each slot shows from the inputs (states, presence, pool members, pins, held slots) and, for the members of apps, the previous result. Its trace is what why and the GUI tell.

  1. Fixed slots. A slot that is held (a finger, a button) or within its dwell time keeps what it shows; its channel or member is reserved, and the other slots are resolved around it.
  2. Channels by rank. Every other slot proposes its first candidate that holds. A channel that several slots propose goes to the one where it is highest in the stack (its rank), ties to the leftmost slot; the others go on to their next candidate that holds. Repeat until nothing changes. A pool takes its slot in this step (pools are not contested). It ends: a slot only goes down its stack.
  3. Pools. The members are handed out:
  4. Chain. The pool a slot shows after step 2, and the candidates below it that hold while they are pools, up to and with a -, form the slot's chain; a channel ends it. ["people if call", "- if call", "auto"] while call is on: people, -.
  5. Hand-down. A pool with no member for a slot leaves it to the next pool of the chain. A slot no pool fills shows the last pool of its chain, dark, or -.
  6. Pools of channels first, in the order of the file, each from its side. One below apps, people or auto in a chain is an error: those take the channels that no pool of channels gives a slot.
  7. Contest. Then the people over the slots whose chain has people or auto, in their order; the applications over those with apps or auto: pins first, then each on the slot it had, then in their order. A slot both fill goes to the one higher in its stack (inside auto, the people); the other gives it up and is handed out again, until nothing changes. It ends: each only loses slots. Members left over wait.
  8. Fill order. Each from its side; the people first take the slots where they are the first candidate, then the others (see Not a block).
  9. A pin reserves its slot if the chain there has apps or auto; a pin waits while a held slot shows its member, and a pin of a channel that has a slot of its own is dropped (one channel, one slot).

Conditions never look at the result, so the steps run once each, in this order: the same inputs give the same result.

Stability¶

  • Batching: changes are resolved together 0.1 s after the first one (runtime/slots.BATCH): a game starts several streams and a process at once, and the faders move once.
  • Dwell: a slot keeps what it shows for at least 1 s before it changes again (slots/simulation.DWELL).
  • Nothing changes under a finger: a held slot is fixed (step 1) until let go, with its claims. A fader in touchless_faders counts as touched while the user moves it and half a second after; the motor moving it doesn't count. The buttons of the chord don't hold slots.
  • Startup: the daemon waits for the first state of the sound server and the first process scan, evaluates the states without delay_on (the baseline), restores the overrides, and only then runs the startup sequence: the first picture is the right one.
  • Reload: the previous result carries over by channel id and member.

Telling what changed¶

A slot that shows something else flashes the new label on the top line and why on the bottom line (the when of the new candidate, or not <when> of the old one) for switching.banner seconds. A new mode shows its label across all LCDs instead. The motor fader moves to the new volume, as with banking in a DAW. The log has one line per change with the reasons.

Controls¶

What a fader, encoder or button of a slot does whatever the slot shows: actions of the place, not of a channel.

[controls]
4 = { encoder = "homeassistant" }                        # number.xtouch_slot_4_encoder and push events
7 = { select = { launch = ["mumble", "mumble://..."] }, when = "not call" }
1 = { solo = "toggle:night", rec = "homeassistant" }
2 = { fader = "homeassistant", id = "desk_fader" }       # number.xtouch_desk_fader_fader
3 = [                                                    # the first whose when holds
    { encoder = "homeassistant", id = "strip_3", when = "gaming" },
    { select = { launch = ["obs"] } },
]
  • What can be claimed: fader, encoder (turn and push), select, rec and solo; not Mute, which stays with what the slot shows, and not the Solo buttons of the chord.
  • Actions: "homeassistant" (a number for the fader or encoder, events for the buttons, a select for their LED), { launch = [...], launch_when_running = ... }, and the actions of the chord: "cycle_modes", "mode:<id>", "toggle:<state>" (the LED shows the state). "toggle:<mode>" picks the mode, and the next press gives it back to the rules; its LED is on while it is the mode. A fader and an encoder only take "homeassistant".
  • One table or a list: the first entry whose when (states only, no present) holds acts. The entries of a slot may share an id; another slot can't have it.
  • A claim wins over what the slot shows and owns its LED or ring; the presenter bound there is told which controls are taken. With the fader taken, the encoder of what the slot shows sets the volume; with only the encoder taken, the fader stays the volume. A candidate can't set the LED (rec, solo) of a claimed button, also while the claim's when doesn't hold: one owner per LED in the file.
  • A channel's own fader/encoder = "homeassistant" (entities by channel id, moving with the channel) stays; a claim of the slot wins while it holds.
  • Controls don't touch the resolver: they hold no slot, and people and channels sit there as anywhere. A release goes to whoever got the press.
  • Entities: *.xtouch_slot_<n>_*, or *.xtouch_<id>_* with id: registered once per configuration, always there, following the slot rather than what it shows.

Pitfalls¶

  • Launch behind a condition. A channel's launch works only while a slot shows it. "voip if call" hides VoIP exactly when you'd press Select to start the voice chat: put the launch into [controls] with when = "not call".
  • Hidden indicators. A hidden channel's LEDs are not on the device: mic if call hides the Mic and its Rec LED, which lights while any application records (a meeting in a browser tab).
  • A voice chat without room. People in a call while no slot is free for them wait; the why line says so ("Bob waits").
  • Rank surprises. 1 = ["buds if present", "out"] and 3 = ["out if video", "auto"]: in video mode without buds, out goes to slot 3 (rank 1 there, 2 on slot 1), and slot 1 shows auto. Correct, but only the outcome table, Simulate or why make it visible.

check-config's notes (slots/outcomes.notes()) name a channel right below a pool without a condition (it never shows) and people with no slot to sit on.
